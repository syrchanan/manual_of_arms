## The three formations, disambiguated (read this first)

Article XIII covers **three distinct results**, all built from the same
8-company line, and it is easy to conflate them. For an 8-company battalion
(companies numbered 1–8, right to left; right wing = 1,2,3,4, left wing =
5,6,7,8; centre companies = 4 and 5):

### 1. Column doubled on the centre ≡ "double column" (¶874–902, and all later
sections that reference it)
**One single column**, formed by folding the whole line inward at its
centre point. Companies are paired by **mirror distance from the centre**:
(4,5), (3,6), (2,7), (1,8). Each pair forms one **division** that is **two
companies wide** (side by side), and the four divisions stack **front to
rear** along one column axis on the former centre line:

```
front  Division 1 = companies 4 | 5      (the original centre companies, stand fast)
       Division 2 = companies 3 | 6
       Division 3 = companies 2 | 7
rear   Division 4 = companies 1 | 8
```

Casey's own name for the result, used interchangeably from ¶889 onward, is
simply "double column" (¶874's heading is the more descriptive "column
doubled on the centre"; ¶889+ paragraph text and all later cross-references
just say "double column" — same formation, not two).

### 2. Division columns (¶903–921, "line of division columns" ¶917+)
**Four separate, parallel columns**, arranged **side by side**, each
preserving its **original lateral (left/right) position** on the line.
Companies are paired by **adjacency**, not mirror distance: right wing pairs
(2 front, 1 rear) and (4 front, 3 rear); left wing pairs (5 front, 6 rear)
and (7 front, 8 rear). Denominated first→fourth **left-to-right beginning at
the right of the line** (¶912):

```
 First           Second           Third            Fourth
 division-column division-column  division-column  division-column
 (rightmost)                                        (leftmost)
 front: 2         front: 4         front: 5         front: 7
 rear:  1         rear:  3         rear:  6         rear:  8
```

Each division-column is only **one company wide, two companies deep** —
geometrically a miniature simple column-by-company, four of which sit
abreast across the original battalion frontage, still separated laterally
by roughly the same intervals as the original companies in line.

### 3. Why "second" and "third" division-columns become "division 1 & 2" of the double column
This is the load-bearing fact that makes ¶928's cross-formation movement
make sense: the **second** division-column (front company 4, rear company
3) and the **third** division-column (front company 5, rear company 6) sit
laterally adjacent to each other at the centre of the line. Their front
companies, 4 and 5, are already abreast at the centre — exactly Division 1
of the double column. Their rear companies, 3 and 6, are already abreast
behind them — exactly Division 2 of the double column. Only the **first**
(rightmost, companies 2/1) and **fourth** (leftmost, companies 7/8)
division-columns are laterally displaced and must close in toward the
centre axis to become Divisions 3 and 4 of the double column.

Conversely, deploying division columns (formation 2) into a line, or
deploying the double column (formation 1) into a line, both end at the same
place (full 8-company line of battle) but travel there via different
per-company paths, per Article XIII's later sections.

Distances/intervals ("at half distance," "closed in mass," "at company
distance," "full distance") are the same single distance-parameter concept
established in Phase B1 (`TODO-battalion-plan.md`), reused here without
new figures except where called out below.

## Section 1 — To ploy the battalion into column doubled on the centre, at a halt (¶874–888)

### Title & paragraph range
"To ploy the battalion into column doubled on the centre." ¶874–888
(lines 4629–4713). Battalion starts in line of battle, at a halt.

### Exact commands
> 1. Double column, at half distance. 2. Battalion, inward—FACE. 3. MARCH
> (or double quick—MARCH). (¶875)

("Closed in mass" is a variant substituting that phrase for "at half
distance," per ¶897 — same commands/mechanics otherwise.)

### Sequence of actions
- ¶874: movement = ploying "corresponding companies of the right and left
  wings" into column at company distance (or closed in mass) in rear of the
  two centre companies, per the general close-column ploy principles of
  Part Second Art. III.
- ¶876 (1st command): every captain steps 2 paces in front of his company.
  Captains of the two **centre companies (4, 5)** caution them to stand
  fast. All other captains caution their own company to face left (right
  wing) or right (left wing). Covering sergeants step into the front rank.
- ¶877 (skirmisher platoon columns forming, `0-`, out of scope).
- ¶878 (2nd command): companies 4 and 5 stand fast. The rest of the right
  wing (1,2,3) faces left; the rest of the left wing (6,7,8) faces right.
  Each captain whose company has faced hastens to break to the rear the two
  files at the head of his company. The **left guide of each right
  company** and the **right guide of each left company** each place
  themselves at the head of the front rank; the captain stands by his
  guide.
- ¶879 (skirmisher platoon columns, `0-`, out of scope).
- ¶880 (command march): companies 4 & 5 (forming Division 1, the *first*
  division of the resulting column) stand fast. The senior of the two
  captains (4, 5) places himself before the centre of the division and
  commands **"Guide right"**; the junior captain takes the interval between
  the two companies; the left guide of company 5 places himself in the
  front rank on the division's left as soon as he can pass.
- ¶881: all other companies, led by their captains, step off briskly to
  arrange themselves in column at company distance — each company falls in
  behind the *preceding* company of its own wing: right wing order (front
  to rear after 4/5) is 3, then 2, then 1; left wing order is 6, then 7,
  then 8.
- ¶882: the corresponding companies of the two wings **unite into
  divisions** as they arrange themselves in column. An instant before each
  union, at the centre of the column, the left guides of the right
  companies pass into the file-closer line; each captain commands: 1.
  *(Such company)*; 2. **Halt**; 3. **FRONT**.
- ¶883: at "Halt," each company halts; at "FRONT," it faces front. In each
  newly formed division, the senior captain places himself on its right and
  commands **"Right—DRESS,"** the junior captain takes the interval between
  the two companies. Once aligned, the division chief commands **"FRONT"**
  and takes post 2 paces before its centre.
- ¶884 (`0-`, skirmisher platoon-column placement — out of scope, but
  establishes the *company-distance* reference figures reused later: platoon
  columns dressed 6 paces in rear of the division next-to-last, 3 paces from
  the outer flanks of the battalion column).
- ¶885: once formed, divisions are numbered **first, second, third,
  fourth… by position in the column beginning at the front** — i.e.
  Division 1 = {4,5} (front), Division 2 = {3,6}, Division 3 = {2,7},
  Division 4 = {1,8} (rear). This confirms the mirror-pairing described in
  the overview above.
- ¶886: the lieutenant-colonel, having placed himself at slightly more than
  company distance in rear of the right guide of Division 1 at the 2nd
  command, assures each division's right guide on direction as it arrives,
  then takes post outside the column's right flank (post No. 175).
- ¶887: the senior major follows the movement abreast with the last
  company, then takes post outside the column's right flank (No. 176). The
  junior major places himself abreast of the first platoon column
  (skirmishers, out of scope), 6 paces outside its right flank.
- ¶888: the music passes to the rear of the column.

### Start/end state
Start: battalion in line of battle, halted, 8 companies. End: single column
of 4 two-company-wide divisions on the former centre axis, at half
distance (or closed in mass per ¶897), guide right, in the mirror-pairing
order given above.

### Distances/measurements
"At half distance" or "closed in mass" (existing distance-parameter
concept, no new figures given here — see ¶897, ¶899 below). Skirmisher
platoon-column figures (6 paces, 3 paces) out of scope.

### Cross-references
Article Third, Part Second (general close-column ploy principles, already
implemented in Phase B1). Post Nos. 175/176 (lieutenant-colonel/senior
major standard column posts, presumably already defined for simple-column
posting in Phase B1).

### Complexity notes
This is the "fold at centre, pair by mirror distance, stack front-to-rear"
primitive described in the overview. See the consolidated **Engine
primitives needed** section at the end of this document for the full
design discussion — this is one of the two foundational new geometric
primitives introduced by this article.

## Section 2 — To form the double column at company distance, without halting (¶889–898), plus mass/distance-change rules and skirmisher remarks (¶899–902)

### Title & paragraph range
Untitled continuation ("The battalion being in march to form the double
column…") ¶889–902 (lines 4714–4794).

### Exact commands
> 1. Double column at half distance. 2. Battalion, by the right and left
> flanks. 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH). (¶889)

### Sequence of actions
- ¶890 (1st command): each captain moves briskly in front of his company's
  centre. Captains of 4 & 5 caution "march straight-forward" and command
  **"Quick time."** All other captains caution their companies to face
  right (right wing) / left (left wing).
- ¶891 (`0-`, skirmisher platoon columns face by the flank while marching —
  out of scope).
- ¶892 (command march): companies 4 & 5 continue straight forward; senior
  captain of the two places himself before the division centre and commands
  **"Guide right"**; junior captain takes the interval; left guide of
  company 5 moves to the division's left front rank; men take touch of
  elbows to the right; color and general guides retake their places. The
  three right companies (1,2,3) face left; the three left companies (6,7,8)
  face right — same file-breaking and guide-repositioning choreography as
  ¶878/¶879 (executed on the march this time).
- ¶893: companies 3 and 6 enter the column and direct themselves parallel to
  Division 1; each other company likewise falls in behind its own wing's
  column, gaining ground toward the head of the column as it goes.
- ¶894: corresponding companies of each wing unite into divisions as they
  take their column positions; each captain, the instant his company's head
  reaches the column centre, commands: 1. *(Such company)* by the right (or
  left) flank. 2. **MARCH.** Senior captain of the pair fronts the division
  centre and commands **"Guide right"**; junior captain takes the interval.
  Once at proper distance, the division chief brings it to quick time.
- ¶895 (`0-`, skirmisher platoon columns face and are conducted per ¶884 —
  out of scope).
- ¶896: with an odd number of companies present, the same method applies;
  any company on either flank without a corresponding pair-mate falls in at
  company distance behind its own wing.
- ¶897: **double column closed in mass** is formed by the identical
  principles/commands, substituting "closed in mass" for "at half
  distance" in the preparatory command.
- ¶898: the double column is never formed when two-or-more battalions must
  join into one general column; it habitually takes the guide to the right,
  sometimes left, or **centre** of the column (command: **"Guide centre"**
  in that case). It marches, countermarches, and changes direction per the
  principles already established for a simple column by division (Phase
  B1).
- ¶899: a double column at company distance closes to mass, or a massed one
  reopens to half distance, by the same commands/means as for a simple
  column (Phase B1 distance-change mechanic, reused as-is).
- ¶900 (`0-`, skirmisher platoon-column repositioning when closing to mass
  or countermarching — out of scope, references No. 425 countermarch
  mechanic).
- ¶901–902 ("Remarks on the disposition of the companies of skirmishers in
  the ployments of the double column") — entirely skirmisher content, out
  of scope.

### Start/end state
Start: battalion marching in line of battle. End: double column (as
Section 1) formed and continuing to march, with no halt at any point in the
transition.

### Distances/measurements
Same as Section 1 — half distance / closed in mass, no new figures.

### Cross-references
No. 878 (referenced implicitly via repeated choreography), No. 884 (platoon
column placement, skirmisher, out of scope), No. 425 and following
(countermarch mechanic, Phase B1 dependency), principles for a simple
column by division (Phase B1, march/countermarch/change-direction/close-
mass/take-distance — all presumed already implemented).

### Complexity notes
Same primitive as Section 1, executed while marching rather than from a
halt — a timing/staging variant, not new geometry, assuming the engine
already models "form X while marching" as a variant entry point the way
Phase B1 did for other ployments.

## Section 3 — To ploy the battalion into division columns, at a halt (¶903–913)

### Title & paragraph range
"To ploy the battalion into division columns." ¶903–913 (lines 4797–4869).

### Exact commands
> 1. Division columns at company distance. 2. Battalion, inward face.
> 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH). (¶904)

### Sequence of actions
- ¶903: movement = ploying the **odd companies of the right wing** (1, 3)
  and the **even companies of the left wing** (6, 8) each in rear of the
  company on its left/right respectively — i.e. 1 behind 2, 3 behind 4, 6
  behind 5, 8 behind 7 (adjacency pairing, not mirror pairing — see
  overview).
- ¶905 (1st command): captains step 2 paces in front of their companies.
  Captains of the **even companies of the right wing (2, 4)** and the
  **odd companies of the left wing (5, 7)** caution "stand fast." Captains
  of the **odd companies of the right (1, 3)** and **even companies of the
  left (6, 8)** caution their companies to face left / right respectively.
  Covering sergeants step into the front rank.
- ¶906 (`0-`, skirmisher company captains cautioned to face — out of
  scope).
- ¶907 (2nd command): companies 2, 4 (right) and 5, 7 (left) stand fast.
  The others (1, 3 right / 6, 8 left) face as cautioned; each captain
  whose company faced hastens right and breaks off files (per No. 111);
  captain and guides place themselves per No. 878's method.
- ¶908 (`0-`, skirmisher faces — out of scope).
- ¶909 (command march): companies 2, 4, 5, 7 stand fast. Captain of the
  **right-centre company (4)** places himself on the right of the front
  rank of his own company; captain of the **left-centre company (5)** on
  the left of the front rank of his own company (both covered in rear rank
  by their guides — note: in this article the two centre companies do
  **not** unite into one division as they do in the double column; each
  stands as the head of its own separate division-column). Captain of
  company 2 takes post front-rank-left of his company; captain of company 7
  takes post front-rank-right of his company (both covered by guides).
- ¶910: all other companies (1, 3, 6, 8), led by their chiefs, step off
  briskly to arrange themselves at company distance: **1 falls in behind
  2, 3 falls in behind 4** (right wing); **6 falls in behind 5, 8 falls in
  behind 7** (left wing). Companies 3 and 6 (the second position in each
  inner division-column) are halted, faced front, and dressed right by the
  senior captain; their captains then take post 2 paces in front of their
  company's centre. Company 1's captain conducts it until its left guide is
  abreast of the left guide of the company (2) in front, then halts,
  fronts, dresses left, posts per No. 169. Company 8's captain conducts it
  symmetrically (right guide abreast of the company (7) ahead, dress
  right).
- ¶911 (`0-`, skirmisher companies file to positions 30 paces behind the
  rearmost division-column's file closers on each flank — out of scope).
- ¶912: the resulting **division columns** are numbered **first, second,
  third, fourth by position in the line, beginning at the right** — i.e.
  DC1 (rightmost) = {2 front, 1 rear}, DC2 = {4 front, 3 rear}, DC3 = {5
  front, 6 rear}, DC4 (leftmost) = {7 front, 8 rear}. With only 7 companies
  present, the 7th company alone stands as a division column; with fewer,
  the movement is not performed.
- ¶913: colonel and junior major posts per Nos. 34–35 (Title I), distances
  measured from the file closers behind the rearmost companies of the
  division columns. Lieutenant-colonel takes post in rear of the centre of
  DC1 (first/rightmost); senior major in rear of the centre of DC4
  (last/leftmost); each 12 paces from those file closers. Adjutant and
  sergeant-major per No. 36 (Title I). Music takes post in rear of the
  **third** division column (DC3), per Nos. 41–42 (Title I).

### Start/end state
Start: battalion in line of battle, halted. End: **four separate,
side-by-side division-columns** at company distance, each 1 company wide
by 2 companies deep, preserving the original left-right ordering of the
line (see overview diagram).

### Distances/measurements
"At company distance" (existing distance-parameter concept). 12 paces
(lieutenant-colonel/senior major offset from file closers), 30 paces
(skirmisher company placement, out of scope).

### Cross-references
No. 111 (breaking off files while facing, presumably a Phase-B1-implemented
detail). No. 878 (double-column captain/guide placement choreography,
reused). No. 169 (standard captain post after dress). Nos. 34–36, 41–42
(Title I field-officer/staff/music posts — presumably already modeled as
part of the base battalion roster).

### Complexity notes
This is the second of the two foundational new primitives: **four
independent, simultaneously-forming 2-company columns, arranged
side-by-side at their original lateral offsets.** Unlike Section 1's
single fold-to-centre column, here nothing converges toward a shared axis
— each division-column's centerline stays at (approximately) its own
pair's original line-of-battle x-position, and only company depth changes
(front company stands, rear company marches up behind it). This is much
closer to running the existing single-column-ploy primitive **four times
in parallel at four different lateral offsets** than to Section 1's fold
primitive. See **Engine primitives needed** at the end of this document.

## Section 5 — To advance in a line of division columns (¶922–923)

### Title & paragraph range
"To advance in a line of division columns." ¶922–923 (lines 4917–4937).

### Exact commands
None new — "by the commands and means prescribed No. 648 and following"
(¶922), i.e. reuses the forward-march/oblique/change-direction commands
from earlier in Part Fifth (out of this range, presumably a Phase B2
dependency).

### Sequence of actions
- ¶922: a line of division columns advances, retires, obliques, or changes
  direction right/left by the existing No. 648+ commands/means, with
  additions: **the captain on the left flank of the leading company of
  DC1**, and **the captain on the right flank of the leading company of
  DC4**, are each responsible for preserving the interval between their
  own column and the adjacent one. When the battalion faces about to
  retire in line: the captains of the two now-leading centre companies (4,
  5) move to the (now-front) rear rank on the companies' outer flanks; the
  leading captains of DC1 and DC4 move to their companies' centre-facing
  flanks, in the (now-leading) rear rank, and take on the distance-keeping
  responsibility described above; captains of the rear companies of each
  division column post 2 paces in front of their own company centre.
- ¶923: at "Forward," the color-bearer and 3 color-guard corporals (file
  closers) place themselves in front of the two leading centre companies,
  opposite their line positions — color-bearer + 2 outer corporals 6 paces
  in front of the file closers; centre corporal + 2 nearest file closers
  cover them in that rank.

### Start/end state
Start/end: line of division columns (4 parallel columns), before and after
advance/retire/oblique/direction-change — formation itself unchanged,
only translating/rotating as a group of 4 independently-tracked columns.

### Distances/measurements
6 paces (color-bearer/corporals ahead of file closers) — otherwise no new
figures.

### Cross-references
No. 648 and following (Part Fifth advance/oblique/change-direction
commands, Phase B2 dependency).

### Complexity notes
Not a new primitive — this is the "four parallel columns" state from
Section 3/4 being marched, obliqued, or wheeled as a group, reusing
whatever translate/wheel primitives already move a line-of-battle-scale
formation, just applied per-column. The one new wrinkle: **which flank
company is responsible for the interval to its neighbor** swaps between
advancing and retiring (a role-reassignment, not a geometry change) —
should be modeled as metadata on the drill's roster assignment, not new
math.

## Section 6 — Passage of obstacles, advancing and retiring in line of division columns (¶924–927)

### Title & paragraph range
"Passage of obstacles, advancing and retiring in line." ¶924–927
(lines 4939–4965).

### Exact commands
> (Such) division column (or columns), obstacle. (¶924, first command by
> the colonel)

Remaining execution uses commands "for breaking off companies in a
deployed line" (cross-reference, not quoted here).

### Sequence of actions
- ¶924: when an obstacle is judged to cover one or more division columns,
  they are broken to the rear per the standard company-breaking-off
  principles (deployed-line context), with the addition: the **leading
  company of the affected division column closes in mass on the last
  company** of the column behind which it marches. Necessary
  break-off commands are given by the **senior captain of the column**,
  not the colonel.
- ¶925: to come back into line, each company is conducted by its chief
  diagonally to the front; when its right file reaches proper distance
  from the division column on the line, it files right, prolongs itself
  parallel to the line, is faced by the flank by its chief's command, and
  takes the step of the line.
- ¶926: if the battalion (advancing in line) is brought to right-about
  after one or more division columns were broken off to the rear, the
  colonel first causes them to **take full distance** before bringing them
  back into line.
- ¶927 (`0-`, skirmisher chiefs/guides execute per No. 654; skirmishers
  preserve relative position with division columns per No. 911 while
  advancing/passing obstacles; retiring-line obstacle passage and
  return-to-line skirmisher behavior per No. 779 — out of scope).

### Start/end state
Start: line of division columns, advancing or retiring, encountering an
obstacle. End: affected column(s) temporarily broken to the rear (massed
behind the column they trail), then rejoined to the line once past the
obstacle.

### Distances/measurements
"Full distance" (existing distance-parameter concept) as the pre-rejoin
state.

### Cross-references
Principles for breaking off companies in a deployed line (likely a Phase
B2 School-of-Company-scale analog — check if implemented). No. 654, No.
779, No. 911 (all skirmisher, out of scope).

### Complexity notes
A temporary-detour variant of Section 5's line-of-division-columns
marching state: one column peels out of line, closes in mass behind its
neighbor, then peels back into line following a diagonal reconnection
path (similar in spirit to existing obstacle-passage drills already built
for simpler formations in Phase B2, generalized to a division-column
unit). Not a new primitive if an obstacle-passage primitive already
exists from Phase B2; otherwise, a moderate lift generalizing that
primitive to operate on 2-company division-column units instead of single
companies.

## Section 7 — To form double column from a line of division columns (¶928–930)

### Title & paragraph range
"To form double column from a line of division columns." ¶928–930
(lines 4967–4984).

### Exact commands
No new commands quoted — reuses Section 1's halted command (No. 875+) or
Section 2's marching command (No. 889+), each "observing what follows."

### Sequence of actions
- ¶928 (from a halt, at half distance or closed in mass): executed by
  Section 1's commands/means (No. 875+), **except**: at the 1st command,
  the captains of the companies of **DC2 (companies 4,3) and DC3
  (companies 5,6)** — i.e. the two centre division-columns — caution them
  to **stand fast** (replacing Section 1's "centre companies 4,5 stand
  fast" instruction, since here 4 companies, not 2, are already

source — the text specifies endpoints (the markers) and general direction
("diagonally to the front") but not the path shape; a straight-line
diagonal interpolation between start and marker position is the

### Complexity notes
A third path to the same double-column target, starting from a simple
column by company rather than a line. The key new detail versus Sections
1/2 is the **serialized, one-pair-at-a-time union with mark-time gating**
(¶992: "the two right companies mark time... until the third has passed,
and the right company will in its turn mark time until the second has
passed") — each successive company must wait its turn before uniting with
its division partner, rather than all four wing-halves marching and
uniting simultaneously as in Sections 1/2. This is exactly the kind of
serial-timing gating the project's `cascadeBlend()` helper (used in Phase
B2's Article XI, form-by-file) was built for — likely directly reusable
here rather than a new primitive. The underlying fold-to-centre geometry
itself (once each company reaches its union point) is the same as Section
1/2/7's, just reached via simple-column-relative starting positions and
gated timing instead of a line-relative simultaneous fold.

## Engine primitives needed (consolidated)

Article XIII is the first place in the project where **more than one
column exists on screen at once**, and it needs new geometric primitives
beyond anything built in Phase B1/B2:

1. **Fold-to-centre column (`columnDoubledOnCentre` / "double column"),
   Sections 1, 2, 7, 12.** Pairs companies by mirror distance from the
   battalion centre — (4,5), (3,6), (2,7), (1,8) — and stacks the pairs
   front-to-rear as 2-company-wide "divisions" along one column axis at
   the former centre-line x-position. Distinct from all existing
   `columnOfCompanies`-family primitives because the marching unit is a
   **pair of companies converging from both wings onto one shared axis**,
   not a single company or an already-adjacent division peeling off a
   line. Three different entry paths reach this same target geometry
   (from line, Sections 1/2; from a line of division columns, Section 7;
   from a simple column by company, Section 12) — each needs its own
   choreography/timing but should converge on **one shared target-state
   function** for the double column itself, parameterized by distance
   (half distance / mass).

2. **Four parallel independent division-columns (`lineOfDivisionColumns`),
   Sections 3, 4.** Pairs companies by simple left-right adjacency — (2,1),
   (4,3), (5,6), (7,8) — and forms each pair into its own miniature
   2-company column *without* changing that pair's lateral (x) position.
   This is close to running the existing single-column-ploy primitive four
   times in parallel at four different x-offsets; likely the **lowest-
   effort new primitive** of the three, if the existing company-pairing/
   ploy-into-column logic from Phase B1 can be parameterized per-offset
   and invoked 4 times.

3. **Fold/unfold transition between the two multi-column formations
   (Sections 7 and 8).** The hardest new work in this article. Section 7
   (division columns → double column) is the easier direction: two of the
   four division-columns are already correctly positioned and simply
   stand fast, while the other two close in diagonally. Section 8 (double
   column → division columns) is the harder, symmetric-opposite direction:
   two divisions stand fast while the two outer divisions **peel apart
   and travel diagonally away from centre to externally-placed markers**
   — the same "bidirectional simultaneous peel" shape already flagged as
   the hardest Phase B1 primitive (`TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s interior-
   division deployment), but here operating on 2-company divisions as
   atomic units, targeting externally-set marker positions (not just
   facing-and-marching-straight), and needing **markers modeled as
   first-class positioned objects** the transition keys off of (lieutenant-
   colonel's and senior major's marker pairs, ¶932). Recommend a dedicated
   `divisionColumnFold()` / `divisionColumnUnfold()` primitive pair rather
   than reusing/stretching an existing one.

4. **Division-wide deployment-into-line (Sections 9, 10, 11).** Structurally
   the existing Phase B1 "deploy column into line" and "right/left into
   line" primitives (Part Fourth), generalized so the marching/wheeling
   unit is a 2-company division rather than a single company. If Phase
   B1's Part Fourth work already parameterized on division-as-unit (the
   plan's own notes say Parts Second/Fourth did), this should be near-
   direct reuse — flag for verification against the actual
   `part-fourth.md` spec/implementation rather than assumed.

5. **Serialized gated merge (Section 12).** Reuses the existing
   `cascadeBlend()` helper (built for Phase B2 Article XI) for the
   one-pair-at-a-time mark-time-gated union of a simple column's
   companies into double-column divisions — not new math, just a new
   call site.

**Net assessment**: two genuinely new primitives (#1 fold-to-centre, #2
parallel four-column) plus one hard new transition (#3 fold/unfold between
them), and three moderate-to-low-effort reuses (#4, #5, and the
marching/no-halt timing variants that recur throughout every section).
This matches the task framing — Article XIII is very likely the single
largest engine-capability lift in the whole School of the Battalion,
concentrated in Sections 1, 3, 7, and 8.

## Flagged ambiguities

- **Section 8's diagonal path shape** (¶936–938) is specified only by
  endpoint (external markers) and general direction ("diagonally to the
  front"), not by an exact angle or intermediate path — a straight-line
  interpolation is the reasonable default, flagged rather than assumed
  authoritative.
- **Section 6's obstacle-passage mechanic** (¶924–927) cross-references
  "the principles prescribed for breaking off companies in a deployed
  line" without giving the mechanic in-range — needs confirmation this
  exists already from an earlier Phase B2 read (Part Fifth Art. I–IV,
  ¶648–743, not covered in `part-fifth-a.md`'s stated range per that
  file's own boundary note — verify against whichever spec actually
  covers ¶648–743).
- **Section 11's ¶981 detachment remark** (DC1/DC4 peeling off entirely to
  attack a flank while DC2/DC3 deploy) reads as a tactical option/aside
  rather than a fixed drill choreography — flagged as a scope question:
  is an optional "detached flank attack" variant wanted, or is the base
  deployment (Section 9/10 reused) sufficient coverage for Section 11?

  884, 891, 895, 900–902, 906, 908, 911, 916, 919, 927, 930, 933, 935,
