## Sub-topic 1 — Change of front perpendicularly forward, on the right company (¶830–842)

### Commands
Setup (no verbal command — physical marker placement, ¶830–831):
> [Colonel places two markers on the new line before the pivot company's new
> position; orders its captain to establish the company against them by a
> wheel to the right on the fixed pivot.]

Execution (¶832):
> 1. Change front forward on first company. 2. By company, right half wheel.
> 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

Mid-movement (¶835, once the colonel judges the wheel sufficient):
> 4. Forward. 5. MARCH. 6. Guide right.

Closing (¶842):
> Guides—POSTS.

### Sequence of actions
- ¶830: colonel decides to change front forward on the **right (first)
  company**, with the old/new lines forming a right angle "or a few degrees
  more or less." Two markers are set on the new direction, in front of
  where the first company will stand; the first company's captain is
  ordered to bring his company onto the markers.
- ¶831: the first company's captain immediately wheels his company to the
  right **on the fixed pivot** onto the markers, halts it, and aligns it by
  the right. (This happens *before* the colonel's spoken command sequence
  below — it is preparatory, done "immediately" on the colonel's private
  order.)
- ¶832: with the pivot company already established on the new line, the
  colonel gives the battalion-wide command (quoted above) to swing the rest
  of the line onto it.
- ¶833: at the first (preparatory) command, **each captain** (of the other
  companies) places himself before the centre of his own company.
- ¶0-834 (skirmishers, out of scope): 1st skirmisher company faces left,
  left guide steps out, captain posts by his guide; 2nd skirmisher company
  cautioned to march straight forward.
- ¶835: at the command of execution (MARCH), **each company** (i.e., every
  company except the already-fixed first) wheels to the right **on the
  fixed pivot**, per the wheel mechanics already defined at S.C. No. 189
  (School of the Company — existing, already-implemented company-level
  wheel). Each company's **left guide** places himself on its left as soon
  as he can get there. When the colonel judges the companies have wheeled
  enough, he commands "4. Forward. 5. MARCH. 6. Guide right."
- ¶836: at "5. MARCH," companies stop wheeling and march straight forward;
  at "6. Guide right," men touch elbows to the right (right-guide dressing).
- ¶837: the **second company's right guide** marches straight forward until
  his company reaches the point where it must turn right to come onto the
  new line; **each succeeding company's right guide** follows in the trace
  of the file immediately ahead of him (i.e., the file that was at the
  cessation-of-wheel position), until that company likewise turns right
  onto the line, after which that guide also marches straight forward. This
  describes a **cascading/staggered turn-onto-line**, company by company
  from the pivot outward — each company travels straight until it reaches
  the spot vacated (in trace) by the company ahead, then turns onto the
  final line itself.
- ¶838: when the **second company** arrives opposite the left file of the
  first company, its captain turns it to the right; the right guide steers
  to arrive squarely on the line of battle; at three paces from the line,
  the captain commands "1. Second company. 2. HALT."
- ¶839: at "HALT," the company halts; files not yet aligned with the guide
  close up promptly; the left guide posts himself on the line of battle;
  once the **lieutenant-colonel** has confirmed his direction, the captain
  aligns the company by the right.
- ¶840: **each following company** (3rd through 8th) repeats what's
  prescribed for the second, in succession, each turning onto the line as
  it reaches the point opposite the left of the company already established
  before it.
- ¶0-841 (skirmishers, out of scope): detailed skirmisher-company
  positioning (30/33-pace offsets) parallel to the battalion companies.
- ¶842: once the whole line is formed, colonel commands "Guides—POSTS,"
  closing the formation (guides return to normal posts).

### Start/end state
Start: battalion halted, in line of battle, facing the old front. Pivot =
first (right) company, already re-established on the new line via its own
preliminary right-wheel before the battalion command is even given. End:
whole battalion in a new line of battle, rotated to intersect the old line
at (approximately) a right angle at the first company's position, all
companies realigned and guides posted.

### Distances/measurements
- Wheel type: "right half wheel" per the command name — but the *actual*
  wheel angle executed by each non-pivot company is **not** a fixed 45°;
  ¶835 has the colonel judge "when the companies have sufficiently
  wheeled" and call Forward/Guide right at that variable moment (see also
  Remarks, ¶871–872, sub-topic 4 below, which explicitly ties wheel amount
  to the old/new angle). "Right half wheel" is the generic *command name*
  for this whole class of forward change-of-front movements (paralleling
  "left half wheel" for the retreat-flank case), not a literal 45°-of-arc
  instruction for every case — treat the command string as fixed text, but
  the wheel geometry itself as angle-dependent, governed by ¶871's rule (see
  sub-topic 4).
- Halt-and-dress distance: companies turning onto the line halt at **three
  paces** from the line of battle (¶838) before final alignment.
- No pace figures given for company-to-company gaps in this sub-topic
  beyond the standard line-of-battle abutting positions (each company
  turns onto the line directly abutting the one before it, per ¶837–838's
  "opposite to the left file" language).

### Cross-references
- S.C. No. 189 (School of the Company — existing wheel-to-the-right-on-
  fixed-pivot mechanic; already implemented at company scale, reused
  verbatim here per ¶835's explicit citation).
- ¶833 and following, re-cited by ¶848 for the marching case (sub-topic 2).

## Sub-topic 2 — Change of front perpendicularly forward, battalion already marching (¶843–851)

### Commands
Setup (no verbal command, ¶843): colonel places two markers on the new
direction before the first company's position, as in the halted case.

Execution (¶843, identical text to ¶832):
> 1. Change front forward on first company. 2. By company, right half wheel.
> 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

Pivot company's own sub-command (¶844):
> 1. Right turn; 2. Quick time.

Mid-movement (¶847, cf. ¶835):
> 4. Forward. 5. MARCH. 6. Guide right.

### Sequence of actions
- ¶843: this variant applies **while the battalion is already marching**
  (in line, presumably straight forward) rather than halted. Colonel sets
  markers as before and gives the same three-part command as ¶832.
- ¶844: at the first (preparatory) command, captains move rapidly to the
  centre-front of their companies. The **first company's captain**
  specifically commands his own company "1. Right turn; 2. Quick time" (a
  distinct sub-drill, S.S. No. 415 — School of the Soldier turn-while-
  marching mechanic). Captains of the other companies merely caution their
  companies that they are about to wheel right.
- ¶0-845 (skirmishers, out of scope): 1st skirmisher captain commands
  "Company by the left flank"; 2nd skirmisher captain cautions straight-
  forward march.
- ¶846: at the command of execution, the **first company** executes a
  marching right turn per S.S. No. 415 (an existing, already-implemented
  School-of-the-Soldier marching-turn primitive — out of this project's
  battalion-scope but presumably already built at company/soldier scale);
  halts three paces short of the markers; files in rear close promptly into
  line; captain aligns the company by the right. This is functionally the
  marching-case equivalent of ¶831's halted preliminary wheel, but executed
  **as part of the same battalion-wide command sequence** rather than
  beforehand (because the battalion is already in motion, the pivot company
  can't pre-stage itself before the command the way it does when halted).
- ¶847: **each other company** wheels to the right on a fixed pivot (same
  as ¶835); left guides post on the left of their companies; colonel judges
  sufficiency and commands "4. Forward. 5. MARCH. 6. Guide right," exactly
  as in the halted case.
- ¶848: these commands (4/5/6) execute exactly as described at ¶833 and
  following (i.e., re-invokes ¶836–840's cascading turn-onto-line
  choreography verbatim — no new mechanic for the marching case beyond the
  differently-executed pivot company).
- ¶849: at the third command (MARCH), briskly repeated by the skirmisher
  company chiefs, the 1st skirmisher company executes what was prescribed
  at ¶834 and ¶841, and the 2nd skirmisher company executes what was
  prescribed at ¶841 (both skirmisher-scope, out of range).
- ¶850: the colonel may instead change front forward **on the eighth (left)
  company**, "according to the same principles and by inverse means" — a
  mirror-image drill (pivot = left flank company instead of right; wheel
  direction reversed).
- ¶0-851 (skirmishers, out of scope): mirrors ¶0-834/¶0-841/etc. for the
  eighth-company case, inverse means.

### Start/end state
Start: battalion marching forward in line of battle, straight direction.
End: battalion marching forward in a new line of battle, wheeled onto the
new direction at the first (or eighth, by inversion) company, same as
sub-topic 1's end state but reached without an intervening halt.

### Distances/measurements
Same three-pace halt-and-dress distance re-invoked from sub-topic 1 (via
¶848's "as indicated No. 833 and following," which pulls in ¶838's 3-pace
figure). No new pace figures introduced in ¶843–851 itself.

### Cross-references
- S.S. No. 415 (School of the Soldier — marching right-turn primitive; not
  in this project's already-implemented set per available context, flag as
  a dependency if not already built for Article X "march by the flank" or
  similar).
- ¶833, 835, 836, 837 ("as indicated No. 833 and following," ¶848) — this
  sub-topic explicitly reuses sub-topic 1's cascading-turn mechanic
  wholesale; it does not redefine it.
- ¶834, 841 (skirmisher paragraphs, re-cited by ¶849, out of scope).

## Sub-topic 3 — Change of front perpendicularly to the rear (¶852–870)

### Commands
Setup (no verbal command, ¶852): colonel communicates his intent privately
to the pivot (right) company's captain; two markers are placed before the
right and left files of that company's *new* (rear-facing) position.

Execution (¶854):
> 1. Change front to the rear, on first company. 2. Battalion, about—FACE.
> 3. By company, left half wheel. 4. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

Mid-movement (¶859, once colonel judges wheel sufficient):
> 5. Forward. 6. MARCH. 7. Guide left.

Second company's halt (¶862, mirrors ¶838):
> 1. Second company; 2. HALT.

Closing (¶866):
> Guides—POSTS.

### Sequence of actions
- ¶852: colonel privately tells the **right company's captain** his
  intent. That captain immediately **faces his company about**, **wheels
  it to the left on the fixed pivot**, halts it when it reaches the
  direction the colonel indicated, faces it **back to the front**, and
  aligns it by the right against two markers the colonel has had placed
  before the right and left files of the new position. (This full
  preliminary sequence — about-face, wheel left, halt, face front, align —
  happens *before* the spoken battalion command, exactly analogous to
  ¶831's forward-case preliminary, but with the extra about-face/re-front
  bracketing since the company ends up facing the opposite direction from
  where it started.)
- ¶0-853 (skirmishers, out of scope): 1st skirmisher company faces right,
  marches forward 33 paces, files left, establishes itself in rear of the
  first battalion company.
- ¶854: colonel gives the four-part command quoted above.
- ¶855: at the second command ("Battalion, about—FACE"), **all companies
  except the right (pivot)** face about.
- ¶0-856 (skirmishers, out of scope): 2nd skirmisher company faces about.
- ¶857: at the third command, the captains of the companies that faced
  about each place themselves **behind** the centre of their company, two
  paces from the front rank — which, since the company has faced about,
  is now the **rear** relative to the original orientation (i.e., captains
  post on what is now functionally their company's new-front side, mirror
  of ¶833's pre-about posting).
- ¶0-858 (skirmishers, out of scope): 2nd skirmisher captain posts the same
  way.
- ¶859: at the fourth command (MARCH), these companies **wheel to the left
  on the fixed pivot, by the rear rank** (i.e., the rank that is now
  leading, since the companies are faced about, functions as the wheel's
  operative rank). Each company's **left guide** — which, post-about-face,
  is the guide who was on the right of the original front rank — places
  himself on the left of what is now the "right" (the former rear rank).
  When the colonel judges the wheel sufficient, he commands "5. Forward.
  6. MARCH. 7. Guide left."
- ¶860: at "6. MARCH," companies stop wheeling and march straight toward
  the new line; at "7. Guide left," they take the touch of the elbow
  toward the left (mirror of ¶836's guide-right).
- ¶861: each company's guide on its right flank — now functionally the
  left guide, given the about-face — conforms to the same cascading-trace
  principle described at ¶837 (follow in the trace of the file ahead until
  the company turns onto the new line).
- ¶862: the **second company from the right**, once opposite the left of
  the first, turns **to the left**; its guide steers to arrive parallel
  with the (new) line of battle, **crosses that line**, and when the
  front rank — now trailing, in the rear, since the companies are faced
  about — is **three paces beyond** the line, the captain commands
  "1. Second company; 2. HALT." (Note the geometric inversion from the
  forward case: here the company deliberately overshoots the final line by
  three paces and will face about again to square up, rather than halting
  short of it.)
- ¶863: at "HALT," the company halts; files not yet in line with the guide
  close up promptly; the captain **faces the company about** (returning it
  to its original forward-facing orientation, now on the new line), then
  aligns it by the right.
- ¶864: **all other companies** (3rd–8th) execute the same as the second,
  each in succession as it arrives opposite the left of the company already
  placed before it on the new line.
- ¶0-865 (skirmishers, out of scope): 2nd skirmisher company executes the
  ¶859/¶860 wheel-and-march, and upon its left file reaching a
  perpendicular with the 7th battalion company's left, turns left, crosses
  the line by 33 paces, halts, faces about, dresses right.
- ¶866: formation complete, colonel commands "Guides—POSTS."
- ¶867: colonel may instead change front to the rear **on the left (eighth)
  company**, "according to the same principles and by inverse means"
  (mirror drill, as in ¶850 for the forward case).
- ¶0-868 (skirmishers, out of scope): mirrors the skirmisher choreography
  by inverse means for the eighth-company case.
- ¶869: general note — the **colonel** gives "general superintendence" to
  the whole movement throughout (both forward and rear change-of-front
  articles; this paragraph is not sub-topic-specific but is textually
  placed at the end of the rear-change sequence).
- ¶870: the **lieutenant-colonel** assures the direction of the guides as
  they successively arrive on the new line of battle, per the same duty
  already prescribed in the prior (forward-case) formations — i.e., ¶870
  is a cross-reference back to the lieutenant-colonel's role in sub-topic
  1 (¶839's "assured in the direction by the lieutenant-colonel"), stated
  once here as a general principle covering both directions.

### Start/end state
Start: battalion halted, in line of battle. Pivot = right (first) company,
which independently about-faces, left-wheels, halts, re-fronts, and aligns
onto the new (rearward-facing, i.e., the line rotated toward what was
previously the battalion's rear) direction before the spoken command.
Every other company faces about, left-wheels on its own fixed pivot (by
the now-leading rear rank), marches onto the new line, overshoots it by
three paces, faces about again, and aligns. End: whole battalion in a new
line of battle, rotated toward the original rear, pivoting at the first
company, all companies front-facing again and aligned, guides posted.

### Distances/measurements
- Wheel type: "left half wheel" (command name, ¶854) — same caveat as
  sub-topic 1: colonel judges the actual wheel amount live (¶859), not a
  literal fixed 45°.
- Overshoot-and-halt distance: companies cross the new line and halt when
  **three paces beyond it** (¶862) — geometrically the mirror of sub-topic
  1's "halt three paces short," because here the operative (leading) rank
  during the wheel is the rear rank, so the company must cross fully onto
  the line before facing about to bring its true front rank onto it.
- Skirmisher offsets: 33 paces (¶0-853, ¶0-865) — out of scope but noted
  for consistency with other 33-pace skirmisher figures elsewhere in this
  Article (¶841, ¶849).

### Cross-references
- ¶837 (cascading-trace guide principle, reused per ¶861).
- ¶833 and following / ¶838 (captain-posting and halt-and-dress mechanics,
  structurally mirrored though not explicitly re-cited by paragraph number
  in this sub-topic — the parallel is described in ¶857/¶862 in the
  rear-case's own words rather than by cross-reference, unlike sub-topic
  2's explicit "No. 833 and following").
- ¶839 (lieutenant-colonel's direction-assuring duty, generalized at ¶870).

## Sub-topic 4 — Remarks on changes of front (¶871–873)

### Commands
Oblique variant (¶872):
> Oblique change of front, forward (or to the rear) on (such) company.

(No further preparatory/execution split given — this is presented as the
single command substituted for "Change front forward/to the rear on
[company]" in the ¶832/¶843/¶854 sequences when the angle is oblique
rather than perpendicular.)

### Sequence of actions
- ¶871: general principle governing **how much** each non-pivot company
  should wheel before marching straight forward, for *any* change of front
  (not just the perpendicular case already drilled in sub-topics 1–3):
  - When the new direction is **perpendicular, or nearly so**, to the old,
    companies "ought to make about a half wheel (the eighth of the
    circle)" before marching straight forward. **Note the source's own
    arithmetic**: it calls this a "half wheel" but glosses that same
    phrase as "the eighth of the circle" — i.e., 45° (an eighth of 360°),
    not half of a 90° perpendicular angle read literally as a fully
    different fraction. Read literally, Casey's "half wheel" here means
    half of a *quarter* wheel (a quarter wheel = 90° = one company facing
    fully onto the perpendicular line), i.e., 45°, consistent with "eighth
    of the circle." This confirms the sub-topic-1/3 reading: the command
    name "right/left half wheel" denotes wheeling roughly 45° before
    straightening out to march onto the line, not the entire perpendicular
    turn.
  - When the old and new lines are **oblique** to each other, "the smaller
    the angle which they form, the less ought the companies to wheel" —
    i.e., the wheel-before-marching-straight amount scales down
    continuously with the obliqueness of the angle.
  - It falls to the **colonel** to judge, by eye, the precise moment to
    give "MARCH" (ending the wheel, starting the straight march) after the
    "Forward" caution — timed to the angle. Casey's explicit guidance:
    "if he cannot catch the exact moment, the word of execution should
    rather be given a little too soon, than an instant too late" (i.e.,
    err toward under-wheeling/early-straightening rather than over-
    wheeling).
- ¶872: for angles of **45 degrees or fewer** between old and new lines,
  the colonel must arrest the wheel **very early** — "when the marching
  flanks shall have taken but a few paces, or... have but disengaged,
  respectively, from the fixed pivots of the next companies" — i.e.,
  almost no wheel at all, just enough for each company's marching flank to
  clear its neighbor's pivot point. In these cases, companies arrive
  "so nearly parallel to the new line" that they can align directly onto
  it **without the intermediate turn to the right or left** described in
  sub-topics 1 and 3 (¶838's/¶862's turn-onto-line step is skipped
  entirely for shallow-angle cases). For this whole class (perpendicular
  *or* oblique, forward *or* rear, whenever the colonel judges an
  intermediate turn unnecessary — though ¶872's own text specifically
  frames this for the ≤45° case), the colonel substitutes the command:
  > Oblique change of front, forward (or to the rear) on (such) company.
  in place of the "Change front forward/to the rear on [company]" wording
  used in ¶832/¶843/¶854.
- ¶873: for oblique changes of front, the skirmisher companies are
  established **parallel to the first and last battalion companies**, by
  the means already explained elsewhere in this Article (out of scope,
  skirmisher-specific).

### Start/end state
Not a standalone drill — this sub-topic is a parametrized **modifier**
governing the wheel-arrest timing and (for shallow angles) whether the
turn-onto-line step is performed at all, applicable to any invocation of
sub-topics 1–3's commands with a non-fixed target angle. Same start/end
states as sub-topics 1 and 3, just reached with a shallower wheel and,
for ≤45° cases, no discrete "turn to the right/left" moment.

### Distances/measurements
- "About a half wheel (the eighth of the circle)" ≈ 45° for the
  perpendicular case (¶871).
- 45° or fewer between old/new lines triggers the minimal-wheel/no-
  intermediate-turn case (¶872) — no lower bound given (could be a very
  shallow angle; the "few paces... disengaged from the fixed pivot"
  language implies the wheel amount continues to shrink toward zero as
  the angle shrinks).
- No exact pace or degree figure is given beyond "the eighth of the
  circle" — everything else in this sub-topic is qualitative ("the
  smaller the angle... the less ought the companies to wheel"), explicitly
  left to the colonel's judgment rather than tabulated.

### Cross-references
- Sub-topics 1–3's own command forms (¶832, ¶843, ¶854), which ¶872's
  oblique command replaces/modifies for shallow angles.
- ¶873 references "the means heretofore explained" for skirmisher
  parallel placement — presumably ¶0-841/¶0-853/etc. within this same
  Article (out of scope).

### Complexity notes
This sub-topic is not independently animatable — it's a continuous
parameter (wheel angle, 0°–~90°, plus a boolean "skip the discrete
turn-onto-line halt for shallow angles") layered onto sub-topics 1 and 3.
Recommend implementing as an optional angle parameter / subMovement
variant on the forward and rear change-of-front drills (e.g., a "45°
oblique" subMovement alongside the primary "perpendicular" one), rather
than a standalone drill file, since ¶871–873 has no independent
choreography of its own — it only modifies timing and the presence/
absence of the ¶838/¶862 turn-onto-line halt.

## Consolidated complexity assessment

**This is a straightforward generalization of the `changeDirectionInLine.js`
(Art. IV) precedent — a rigid `wheel()` cast about a single flank pivot —
for the core mechanic, but with real additional choreography layered on
top that Art. IV did not need.** Specifically:

1. **Core rotation is the same primitive.** Both sub-topic 1 (forward,
   right-company pivot) and sub-topic 3 (rear, right-company pivot, with
   ¶850/¶867 inverse-means mirrors onto the left/eighth company) are, at
   the whole-battalion level, exactly what Art. IV already models: the
   entire line-of-battle cast rotated rigidly about one flank individual's
   position, using the existing `wheel()` function from
   `src/engine/formations.js`. No new rotation math is needed.

2. **New wrinkle not present in Art. IV: the pivot company moves
   independently and *first*, via its own separate wheel, before the
   battalion-wide command even fires** (¶831 for forward, ¶852 for rear).
   Art. IV's pivot (the flank captain) is a literal fixed point the whole
   cast rotates around; here, the pivot *company itself* physically wheels
   into its new position as a preliminary, private-order step, and *then*
   becomes the fixed anchor for the rest of the line's wheel. This is a
   two-stage choreography (stage A: pivot company self-positions via its
   own company-scale wheel — reusing the already-implemented
   School-of-Company wheel primitive per ¶835's explicit "S.C. No. 189"
   citation; stage B: whole-battalion wheel about that now-fixed company,
   using the battalion-scale `wheel()`), not a single-stage rigid rotation.

3. **New wrinkle: cascading, staggered turn-onto-line per company
   (¶837–840, mirrored ¶861–864), not a single simultaneous halt.** After
   the initial wheel phase, each company (2nd through 8th, working outward
   from the pivot) continues straight-marching independently, at its own
   pace, until it reaches the position vacated by the company ahead of it,
   then executes its own discrete turn (right for forward/¶838, left for
   rear/¶862) and halt-and-dress. This is NOT simultaneous across the
   line — it's serial/cascading, timed by each company's own arrival at a
   geometric trigger point (opposite the left file of the company already
   placed). This closely resembles the `cascadeBlend()` serial-timing
   primitive already built for Part Fifth Article XI (form by file into
   line, per `TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s Phase B2 notes) — **recommend
   reusing/adapting `cascadeBlend()` for this staggered turn-onto-line
   phase** rather than building a third serial-timing mechanic.

4. **New geometric detail specific to the rear case (sub-topic 3): the
   about-face bracket and the three-pace *overshoot* (vs. sub-topic 1's
   three-pace *undershoot*).** Because the operative rank during a
   rear-change wheel is the rear rank (companies have faced about), each
   company must cross fully past the new line by three paces before
   facing about again to bring its true front rank onto the line (¶862–
   863) — geometrically the mirror image of the forward case's "halt
   three paces short" (¶838), not merely a sign-flipped wheel direction.
   Needs its own explicit overshoot/re-about-face keyframe step, not
   reducible to reusing the forward case's keyframes with a negated angle.

5. **Sub-topic 4 (Remarks) is a continuous-parameter modifier, not new
   choreography** — implement as an angle parameter and a "skip the
   discrete turn" boolean on sub-topics 1/3's drill(s), per the complexity
   note there, not as a fifth standalone drill file.

6. **Color guard / field-and-staff posts are NOT specified anywhere in
