Source: `casey_v2_full_extract.txt`, Title V "School of the Battalion," Part
Third (heading at file line 1172). Article boundaries confirmed by grepping
`ARTICLE` headings within Part Third: Art. I line 1174, Art. II line 1331,
Art. III line 1495, Art. IV line 1578, Art. V line 1623, Art. VI line 1818,
Art. VII line 1857 (¶343 — first paragraph outside this range, confirming
Art. VI/¶342 is the correct end boundary for this spec).

## Key definitions (established before/within this range — read carefully, these are load-bearing for the whole Phase B1 engine)

- **Full distance**: the interval between successive subdivisions (company,
  division, or platoon) in column equals **the front (width) of one
  subdivision of that column's unit-of-formation**. Established generically
  in Part Second (¶117: a platoon breaking into column marches "a distance
  equal to the front of his platoon" before halting/filing past; ¶168:
  divisions ploying into column are separated "the distance of four paces" —
  see Complexity Notes below on this apparent inconsistency). Within this
  range, ¶294 explicitly calls a column "at full distance" the baseline state
  before closing.
- **Half distance**: the interval is reduced to **one platoon's front**
  (i.e., half of a company's front) — directly demonstrated at ¶298: when
  closing a column-by-company to half distance, each following company halts
  "as each in succession arrives at **platoon distance** from the one which
  precedes." So "half distance" for a company column = platoon-front spacing
  between company guides.
- **Closed in mass**: subdivisions closed up until only a minimal gap
  remains for file closers — defined at ¶333 as **six paces** between the
  guides of successive subdivisions ("he will not halt his subdivision till
  its guide shall be at a distance of six paces from the guide of the
  subdivision next preceding"). Note ¶168 (Part Second, ploying divisions
  into *close column*) uses **four paces** between division guides instead —
  flagged as an unresolved cross-reference/discrepancy, not chased (see
  Complexity Notes, Art. V).
- These three states are a single **distance parameter** on the column, not
  three separate formations: full → half → mass is a monotonic closing-up of
  the same column structure, executed by the same commands/roles with only
  the halting-distance criterion changed (¶332, ¶333).

## Article I — ¶216–232: "To march in column at full distance"

**Commands** (verbatim):
- ¶219: `1. Column forward. 2. Guide left (or right). 3. MARCH (or double quick–MARCH).`
- ¶225: `1. Battalion, right about. 2. MARCH. 3. Guide right.` (about while marching)

**Sequence of actions:**
- Colonel indicates two distant objects on the line of march to the leading
  guide before starting (¶216); if only one object, guide catches an
  intermediate point himself (¶217); if no object at all, colonel sends the
  lieutenant-colonel or adjutant 40 paces in advance to mark the direction by
  sword-sign (¶218), guide conforms per S.C. No. 87 (cross-ref, not chased).
- At `MARCH`, briskly repeated by chiefs of subdivision and chiefs of platoon
  of skirmisher companies, the column steps off per S.C. No. 205ff (¶220,
  cross-ref).
- Leading guide keeps the two points aligned (nearer masks farther) to hold
  direction (¶221). **Following guides do not attend to general direction at
  all — each follows exactly in the trace of the guide immediately ahead**
  (¶222); this is reiterated as the cardinal marching principle (¶234–235).
- Lieutenant-colonel rides/walks abreast of the leading guide, watching for
  deviation, and also checks that the second guide traces the first (¶223).
- Senior major stays abreast of the last subdivision, correcting guide drift
  before it propagates down the column, but only intervening on "sensible or
  material" deviation (¶224); junior major's post per ¶94 (cross-ref, not
  chased).
- **Column about while marching** (¶225–226): on `Battalion, right about /
  MARCH`, companies face right-about and the column reverses direction in
  place — chiefs of subdivision stay behind the (new) front rank, file
  closers move in front of the (new) rear rank, guides relocate to what is
  now the front rank. Skirmisher platoon columns' second platoons march
  abreast of the first/last battalion companies. Lieutenant-colonel now
  trails abreast of the first division (now in rear); senior major gives the
  new leading guide a point of direction and marches abreast of him.
- Colonel holds himself on the directing flank throughout, watching step and
  distances per School of the Company principles (¶227).
- **Prolonging a line of battle by markers** (¶228–231): when no natural
  landmarks exist for guides to march on, mounted officers are posted on the
  intended line as artificial aiming points. Three-officer relay method
  (¶229): officers post at the column's entry point, then 300–400 paces
  behind, then another 300–400 paces behind that; as the head of column
  passes each, that officer gallops around to re-post beyond the rearmost.
  Single officer suffices when the point of direction is already distinct
  (¶230). For a column of one or two battalions, dismounted markers suffice
  (¶231).
- **Remarks** (¶232): full distance is not sustained long except in route
  step or ceremony/review; in presence of the enemy the column is habitually
  at half distance or closed in mass. (¶233–235 restate route-step rationale
  and the "follow the guide ahead, not the general direction" principle —
  already captured above.)
- ¶236: diminishing/increasing front by platoon (company↔division front
  changes while marching at full distance) executed per S.C. Nos. 287, 278ff
  with company↔platoon terms substituted — cross-ref, not chased.
- ¶237: column at a halt, small rearward move not worth a countermarch —
  face the whole column about and march per ¶219 commands; in a column by
  division, junior captains in company intervals swap places with covering
  sergeants (junior captain takes the front-rank interval post, sergeant
  moves to file-closer line).
- ¶238 (numbered "0-238", a skirmisher-specific note): skirmisher companies
  keep their relative position in the column through all these movements;
  platoon guides always stay on the column's side (flank).

**Start/end state:** Column already formed (from Part Second) at full
distance, at a halt, facing the intended direction of march → column
marching steadily at full distance, guides tracking a point of direction
(or reversed 180° via the about-while-marching variant), still at full
distance throughout.

**Distances:** Full distance only (no change of distance in this article).
40 paces = lieutenant-colonel/adjutant's advance post when marking direction
with no landmark (¶218). Marker relay spacing: 300–400 paces (¶229).

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** S.C. No. 87 (¶218), S.C. No. 205ff
(¶220), No. 94 (¶224, junior major's post), S.C. Nos. 287 and 278ff (¶236),
No. 219 self-reference (¶237).

**Complexity notes:** The about-face-while-marching (¶225–226) is a
column-wide in-place reversal, not a simple animation loop — guides,
chiefs, and file-closers all swap front/rear roles simultaneously, which is
functionally identical to the Lesson V "about face" work already done at
company scale, just replicated per-company plus a whole-column role-swap for
lieutenant-colonel/senior major posts. The marker-relay mechanic (¶229) is
pure staging/reference geometry (mounted officers as moving waypoints) —
likely out of scope for animation (administrative aid, not troop movement)
but worth a note/annotation layer if the project wants to visualize "points
of direction."

## Article II — ¶233–272: "Column in route"

Note on boundary: TOC pointed to "No. 239" as an approximate start, but the
actual `ARTICLE II` heading (line 1331) falls immediately after ¶238, at
¶239's heading "Column in route" — confirmed exact: **Article II = ¶239–272**
(¶233–238 are the tail end of Article I's "Remarks on the march in column,"
per the text's own paragraph flow — they appear before the `ARTICLE II`
heading in the file, so they belong to Article I, not II. The TOC's "No.
239" for this article is exactly correct.)

**Commands** (verbatim):
- ¶250: `1. By section (or by platoon) into line. 2. MARCH.`

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶239: a route column should never be deeper than the front it occupied in
  line of battle, minus one subdivision's front.
- ¶241: a route column is habitually formed **by company**.
- Defile-narrowing ladder, executed as the way narrows (¶242–248):
  company → platoon (¶242) → (short defile: break a few files to the rear
  instead, ¶243) → section, if platoons have ≥12 files (¶244–245) → break 1–2
  files to the rear if platoons have <12 files, continuing "as long as six
  files can march abreast" (¶246, generalizes to sections too per ¶247) →
  if narrower than 6 abreast, subdivisions march successively **by the
  flank** per S.C. Nos. 319–320 (¶248, cross-ref).
- Re-forming as the defile widens, by captains' own commands as their
  companies individually clear it (¶249): at 6-abreast width, `By section (or
  by platoon) into line. MARCH` (¶250) — files that couldn't yet enter line
  follow by the flank behind the last 4 files of their subdivision that did
  enter (¶251); more files enter line as width increases (¶252); full
  platoons/companies re-form as width allows (¶253).
- Leading subdivision follows the defile's actual windings; trailing
  subdivisions don't independently navigate — each just retraces the path of
  the one ahead (¶254); men don't dodge bad footing individually, they stay
  in file (¶254).
- Changes of direction in the defile happen **without command** — a mere
  caution from each chief to their subdivision suffices for an "important"
  change; rear rank and any files broken to the rear execute the same
  movement at the same point the front rank did (¶255).
- Colonel stays at the head of the battalion, sets the pace of the lead
  subdivision, and times the various movements (¶256).
- Multi-battalion column: each battalion conforms in turn to what the lead
  battalion did, at the same place, same manner (¶257).
- ¶258: practice recommendation (route step drilled during marches to/from
  the drill ground) — administrative, not a maneuver.
- ¶259 (numbered "0-259"): skirmisher companies post at head (1st company)
  and rear (2nd company) of the column on marches; junior major posts
  abreast of the color company, 6 paces from its reverse flank.
- **General remarks on column in route** (¶260–272):
  - ¶261: ordinary route pace = **110 paces/minute** on good ground.
  - ¶263–264: a subdivision never takes more than the prescribed distance
    from the one ahead, but may close it up (even to mass) temporarily around
    an obstacle, then recover distance afterward; if that's not recoverable
    without running, the chief halts the lead subdivision beyond the
    obstacle at a distance sufficient to hold the whole column in mass, then
    resumes march with subdivisions taking distance by the head (front-to-back
    recovery), timed so the last subdivision needn't halt after clearing.
  - ¶265: rate changes ripple rearward — lead battalion changes pace
    insensibly, each following battalion regulates itself on the one ahead.
  - ¶266: multi-battalion column — an aide stays with the rear for prompt
    word if it's struggling to keep up.
  - ¶267: subdivisions must step out briskly when obliquing to break/form
    companies/platoons in succession, so as not to elongate the column.
  - ¶268: flank march hitting a 2-men-wide defile: colonel orders support
    arms, resumes cadenced step, **undoubles files** per S.C. No. 331; files
    **re-double** once the way widens again.
  - ¶269: a 1-man-wide defile: men pass one at a time per S.C. Nos. 334–335;
    reform into 2 or 4 ranks per S.C. Nos. 336–339 once width allows, then
    into platoons/sections at ¶250's threshold.
  - ¶270: after either defile case, the column head marches on until there's
    room to hold the whole column in mass, then resumes per ¶264's method.
  - ¶271: rapid movement over distance is done at double-quick, but capped at
    **15 minutes double-quick / 5 minutes ordinary route step**, alternating;
    double-quick reserved for favorable ground on uneven terrain.
  - ¶272: alternating this way covers long distances quickly; but for ≤2
    miles on good ground, march at one constant rate throughout.

**Start/end state:** Column marching in route step (uncadenced), width
adapting company → platoon → section → file-by-flank and back as terrain
requires; column reforms to full company front once clear.

**Distances:** Route column depth ≤ line-of-battle front minus one
subdivision's front (¶239). ≥12 files required to further diminish
platoon→section (¶244). "Six files abreast" is the minimum before flank-march
is required (¶246, ¶248). Route pace: 110 paces/min (¶261). Double-quick
capped at 15 min on / 5 min ordinary route step off (¶271). Constant-rate
threshold: 2 miles (¶272).

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** S.C. Nos. 319–320 (¶248), S.C. No.
331 (¶268), S.C. Nos. 334–335, 336–339 (¶269).

**Complexity notes:** This article is the most novel relative to existing
School-of-Company engine work: it's a **dynamic width-adaptation state
machine** (company → platoon → section → file, and back), not a fixed
column shape. If animated at all (this reads more like a doctrine/reference
article than a single discrete drill with fixed start/end keyframes — it
describes a continuum of responses to terrain, not one movement), it would
need a "defile width" parameter driving which of ~5 discrete states the
column is in, plus the undouble/re-double file mechanic (already proven at
company scale per project history, per S.C. No. 331/¶363 precedent noted in
CLAUDE.md). Likely a lower priority / reference-only entry rather than a
core animated drill, given its process-description character.

## Article III — ¶273–285: "To change direction in column at full distance"

**Commands** (verbatim):
- ¶273: `Head of column to the left (or right).`
- ¶279: `1. By the right flank. 2. By file right.` (chief of first platoon
  column, when skirmisher platoon columns are present)
- ¶280 ("0-280"): `1. Mark time. 2. MARCH.` (second platoon's chief, halting
  it level with the wheeling point)

**Sequence of actions:**
- Colonel goes in advance to the point where the change should begin and
  posts a **marker** there, breast to the flank of the column, standing on
  the side **opposite** the direction of the turn; marker holds position
  until the whole battalion has passed (¶273).
- Lead subdivision nears the marker → colonel commands `Head of column to
  the left (or right)` (¶273).
- Chief of the leading subdivision takes the guide on the side opposite the
  turn (if not already posted there); that guide aims to graze the marker's
  breast; on reaching it, the chief wheels the subdivision per School of
  Company commands/principles; once the wheel completes, the chief retakes
  the guide on the side of the (new) primitive direction (¶274).
- Every succeeding subdivision's chief and guides repeat exactly what the
  leading subdivision did (¶275).
- Colonel watches that each subdivision's guide, while wheeling, stays
  exactly on the arc (neither cutting inside nor swinging outside) (¶276).
- If no landmark exists for the new direction, lieutenant-colonel posts
  himself on it 30–40 paces from the marker, confirmed by the colonel; the
  leading guide, the moment he's turned, sights two ground points in line
  with the lieutenant-colonel's heels, refreshing them as he advances (¶277).
- Senior major watches that guides aim to graze the marker's breast (¶278).
- **Skirmisher platoon columns present** (¶279–284, all numbered "0-"
  paragraphs except 279 itself is plain-numbered but content is skirmisher
  mechanics): on `Head of column to the right`, the first platoon column's
  chief commands `By the right flank. By file right`, chiefs/guides take
  post on the right of their platoons (¶279). At `MARCH` from the first
  battalion company's captain, platoons file past their (standing-fast)
  chief; when the left file draws level, chief commands `Mark time. MARCH`,
  faces the platoon to the front, guide moves to its left; the first platoon
  angles diagonally to the rear and files into column parallel to the
  second; process repeats for the second platoon (¶280). When the first
  battalion company reaches the first platoon, that platoon column resumes
  march, guide left, regaining 33 paces distance from the battalion column's
  right flank (¶281). When the second platoon column reaches 33 paces from
  the new-direction right flank, it changes direction to the right by its
  own chief's command; its first platoon marches abreast of the last
  battalion company when that company turns (¶282). If the turn is to the
  left instead, the first platoon column takes the guide right and
  increases gait to change direction simultaneously with the first
  battalion company, maintaining relative position/distance, then resumes
  battalion pace (¶283). When the last battalion company turns, the second
  platoon column mirrors what was just prescribed for the first (¶284). In
  a multi-battalion column, the second battalion's lieutenant-colonel has
  the first battalion's marker replaced once that battalion's last
  subdivision clears it, and this repeats battalion by battalion to the
  rear (¶284).

**Remarks** (¶285): reiterates (from School of Company) why each subdivision
must wheel exactly where the leading one did, squared to the new direction,
and must clear the wheeling point promptly so as not to block the next
subdivision — the deeper the column, the more rigorously this must be
observed, since a minor fault compounds with depth.

**Start/end state:** Column marching at full distance in one direction →
column marching at full distance in the new direction, each subdivision
having independently wheeled at the same fixed ground point (the marker).

**Distances:** 30–40 paces (lieutenant-colonel's post confirming new
direction, ¶277). 33 paces (skirmisher platoon columns regaining/holding
distance from the battalion column's flank, ¶281–282).

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** "school of the company" wheel
commands/principles generically invoked (¶274, ¶285) rather than cited by
number — this is the direct battalion-scale reuse point for the project's
existing `wheel()` engine function.

**Complexity notes:** This is the cleanest reuse of existing engine work:
a **fixed-point wheel repeated identically by each subdivision** in
sequence down the column — directly analogous to Lesson V's column-wheel
work, just repeated N times (once per company) at the same ground-fixed
marker rather than each company wheeling at its own trailing point. The
marker itself (a stationary reference actor) is a new renderable entity not
yet in `constants.js`'s soldier/officer token set — likely a small "marker"
glyph, or reuse of an existing officer marker token. Skirmisher platoon
column geometry (¶279–284) is significantly more complex (diagonal filing,
distance-regaining, asymmetric left/right turn handling) — flag as
optional/deferred detail if skirmisher companies aren't in the v1 battalion
model (per TODO-battalion-plan.md, no mention yet of skirmisher companies
in the 8-company default model — confirm before implementing this sub-case).

## Article IV — ¶286–293: "To halt the column"

**Commands** (verbatim):
- ¶286: `1. Column. 2. HALT.`
- ¶291: `Guides, cover.`
- ¶292: `Left (or right)--DRESS.`
- ¶290 (not a drill command per se but a quoted directive): `Guide of (such)
  company, or guides of (such) companies, to the right (or to the left)`

**Sequence of actions:**
- `Column. HALT` — briskly repeated by captains and by skirmisher platoon
  chiefs; column halts; **no guide moves to fix drift or distance loss** —
  they halt exactly where they are (¶286–287).
- Same commands halt a column marching in double-quick; men individually
  fix their own ranks-position after halting (¶288).
- To then form line of battle from the halted column: colonel moves
  slightly ahead of the leading guide and faces him; that guide and the one
  behind fix their eyes on the colonel to read his direction cues (¶289).
- If a general realignment isn't needed, colonel individually corrects only
  the guides that are out of line via the named-company command (¶290);
  named guides move to the line, others stand fast.
- If a **general** direction is wanted: colonel places the first two guides
  himself on the chosen line, commands `Guides, cover` — remaining guides
  cover the first two in file, each exactly one company-front's distance
  from the guide ahead; lieutenant-colonel assures them on the line (¶291–292).
- Colonel commands `Left (or right)--DRESS`: briskly repeated by chiefs of
  subdivision; each company inclines and dresses forward/backward to bring
  the designated flank onto its guide; captain posts 2 paces outside his
  guide, aligns his company parallel to the one ahead, commands `FRONT`,
  returns to his post (¶293).

**Start/end state:** Column marching (any distance/gait) → column halted,
guides at rest (not individually corrected) → optionally, guides
individually or generally realigned and companies dressed square to a
common line, still in column formation (this article does NOT deploy into
line of battle — that's Part Fourth; this is just squaring up the halted
column's guide-line).

**Distances:** None new — reuses "company-front" spacing for guide-covering
(¶292), same concept as full-distance definition above.

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** none explicit; ¶289's "form it into
line of battle" gestures at Part Fourth's deployment content without citing
a paragraph number.

**Complexity notes:** Straightforward — halt is a single freeze-frame
keyframe; the guide-dress sub-sequence (¶290–293) is an optional
"straighten up the column" cosmetic keyframe, useful for a "dress the column
at the halt" micro-drill but not required for basic column marching. No new
engine capability needed beyond existing halt/dress logic from Company
work.

## Article V — ¶294–334: "To close the column to half distance, or in mass"

**Commands** (verbatim):
- ¶294: `1. To half distance, close column. 2. MARCH. (or double quick--MARCH).`
- ¶310: `Double quick--MARCH.` (resuming gait once rearmost company gains distance)
- ¶316: `1. On the eighth company, to half distance, close column. 2.
  Battalion about--FACE. 3. Column forward. 4. Guide right. 5. MARCH (or
  double quick--MARCH).` (closing on the **rear/8th** company instead of lead)
- ¶325: `1. On the eighth company, to half distance, close column. 2.
  Battalion right about. 3. MARCH (or double quick--MARCH). 4. Guide right.`
  (same, executed while already marching)
- ¶333: substitute phrase `column, close in mass` for `to half distance close
  column` to close all the way to mass instead of half distance.

**Sequence of actions — closing on the leading company (¶294–311):**
- On the preparatory command, the leading company's captain cautions it to
  stand fast (¶295); (if skirmisher platoon columns present) the first
  platoon of the first platoon column is likewise cautioned to stand fast
  (¶296, "0-296").
- On `MARCH` (repeated by all captains except the lead one): lead company
  stands fast, its chief aligns it by the left, file closers close one pace
  onto the rear rank (¶297). All other companies keep marching; each halts
  as it reaches **platoon distance** from the company ahead of it (¶298).
  As each halts, its guide lines up on the guides ahead, and its captain
  aligns it by the left with file closers closing one pace (¶299). Guides
  need not worry about general alignment before halting — just trace the
  one ahead (¶300).
- Skirmisher platoon-column mirroring (¶301–302, "0-301"/"0-302"): first
  platoon of the first platoon column stands fast, second platoon "closes
  in mass" onto it (note: mass, not half-distance, for this internal
  skirmisher-platoon closure) and aligns by the left; when the last
  battalion company halts, the second platoon column's second platoon
  closes in mass on its own first platoon.
- Colonel superintends from the guide side, checking each captain halts
  exactly at platoon distance (¶303). Lieutenant-colonel, a few paces
  ahead, faces the leading guide and confirms each following guide's
  position as it's taken (¶304). Senior major follows abreast of the last
  guide; junior major follows abreast of the color company (¶305).
- Same commands close a **marching** column (¶306). In double-quick: at the
  preparatory command the lead captain commands `quick time` for his own
  company while others are cautioned to keep marching double-quick (¶307,
  and the mirrored skirmisher version ¶308 "0-308"); at `MARCH`, lead
  company marches quick time while the rest continue double-quick, each
  dropping to quick time upon reaching platoon distance from the one ahead
  (¶309, mirrored for skirmishers ¶311 "0-311", threshold **six paces**
  there for the skirmisher case). Once the rearmost gains its distance,
  colonel may resume double-quick for the whole column via `Double
  quick--MARCH` (¶310).
- Halting and closing simultaneously (¶312–315): colonel pre-notifies the
  lead captain and lead skirmisher-platoon chief of intent; at `MARCH`, lead
  company halts and aligns by the left (¶312, mirrored ¶313 "0-313" for
  skirmishers, "dress" not "align" language used there). If marching in
  quick time and no double-quick is added, same halt-and-align at `MARCH`
  (¶314, incl. captains' behavior per ¶307/¶309 if double-quick is added).
  Lead skirmisher-platoon chief conforms identically (¶315, "0-315").

**Closing on the rearmost (8th) company instead (¶316–331):**
- At a halt (¶316–324): preparatory command `On the eighth company, to half
  distance, close column` plus `Battalion about-FACE`; 8th company's captain
  cautions it to stay facing front (¶317); all other companies face about
  (guides staying in what's now the rear, formerly front, rank) (¶317,
  mirrored for skirmisher first-platoon-column chiefs ¶318 "0-318", whose
  second-platoon-column counterparts stand fast instead). At `Guide right`
  (4th command), all captains post 2 paces outside their companies on the
  directing flank (¶319, mirrored ¶320 "0-320" for skirmisher platoon
  chiefs). At `MARCH`: 8th company stands fast, aligns by the left, file
  closers close a pace; other companies march (now effectively rearward
  since they're faced about) and each halts+fronts at platoon distance from
  the company already fixed ahead of it; the halting company's left guide
  (still faced to rear) plants itself on the established guide-line, then
  faces about is corrected once the captain aligns by the left and closes
  file closers a pace (¶321). In double-quick, each captain instead commands
  `Such company, right about--HALT` to halt+face the designated company
  (¶321). Skirmisher second-platoon-column mechanics mirrored at ¶322
  ("0-322"), with a specific interval formula: "difference between the
  platoon front and six paces" governs when the second platoon of the first
  platoon column is halted/faced about. Once fully aligned, colonel has the
  rear-faced guides face about (¶323). Lieutenant-colonel posts behind the
  rearmost guide, confirming each guide's position in turn (as in ¶304);
  senior major stays abreast the rearmost company; junior major keeps the
  ¶305 post (¶324).
- While marching (¶325–331): preparatory command `On the eighth company, to
  half distance, close column` + `Battalion right about` + `MARCH` (+`Guide
  right` as 4th); 8th company's captain cautions it to stay facing front,
  others caution their companies they'll face about (¶326, mirrored for
  skirmisher platoon chiefs ¶327 "0-327", opposite roles for the two platoon
  columns). At `MARCH`: 8th company halts, aligns left, file closers close a
  pace (¶328); other captains post on the column flank, subdivisions face
  about (guides remaining in what's now the rear rank), each halts+fronts at
  platoon distance from the company fixed ahead, guide re-establishes the
  line, captain aligns and closes file closers (¶329, mirroring ¶321's
  mechanics). Skirmisher second-platoon-column mirrored at ¶330 ("0-330").
  Lieutenant-colonel follows abreast the first company; senior major posts a
  few paces behind the 8th company's guide, confirming other guides in turn;
  junior major keeps the ¶305 post (¶331).

**Remarks (¶332–334):**
- ¶332: a column **by division** at full distance closes to half distance
  by the identical means/commands as by company.
- ¶333: a column by company **or** division, at full **or** half distance,
  closes **into mass** by the same means/commands, substituting `column,
  close in mass` for `to half distance close column`; the only mechanical
  difference: each chief does NOT halt his subdivision until its guide is
  **six paces** from the guide of the subdivision ahead (rather than
  platoon-distance).
- ¶334: in a column left-in-front, these movements execute on the same
  principles (mirrored handedness).

**Start/end state:** Column at full distance (halted or marching, lead-first
or rear-first/reversed) → same column closed to half distance (guides
separated by one platoon-front) or to mass (guides separated by 6 paces),
same order of companies, same direction of march resumed if applicable.

**Distances:** Half distance = platoon-front interval between guides
(¶298–299). Mass = **6 paces** between guides (¶333) generally, but note
the skirmisher-specific double-quick case at ¶311 also uses "six paces" as
its threshold — and separately, ¶168 (Part Second, outside this range) used
**4 paces** for divisions ploying directly into close column. This project
should treat ¶333's 6-pace figure as authoritative for the "closing an
existing full/half-distance column into mass" maneuver covered here, and
flag ¶168's 4-pace figure as a distinct initial-ploy case to resolve later
if divisions-into-mass geometry is needed (cross-reference, not chased here).

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** ¶259 (junior major's route-column
post, referenced again at ¶336 in Art. VI), Nos. 304–305 self-references
throughout.

**Complexity notes:** This is the article that makes "distance" a genuine
**parameter** on the column formation function rather than a boolean —
full → half → mass are three magnitudes of the same guide-to-guide gap
(company-front / platoon-front / 6 paces), and the engine needs a
`columnOfCompanies(companies, { distance: 'full'|'half'|'mass' })`-style
API (matches TODO-battalion-plan.md's proposed `columnOfCompanies` stub).
The "close on the rearmost/8th company" variant (¶316–331) is a distinct,
more complex sub-drill: it requires an about-face of the whole column
first, individual companies halting-and-refacing at computed intervals
while guides temporarily sit in the "wrong" (rear-becomes-front) rank, then
a final face-about of just the guides. This is meaningfully harder than the
lead-company case and should probably be its own drill entry, not a
variant flag, given the amount of distinct choreography (¶317–324 alone is
nearly as long as the entire lead-company case). Double-quick timing
variants (¶307–311, ¶326–331) add a third dimension (gait) to the
distance-closing keyframes — likely representable as intermediate
keyframes with a faster per-company halt stagger rather than new geometry.

## Article VI — ¶335–342: "To march in column at half distance, or closed in mass"

**Commands:** None newly verbatim-quoted in this article — it explicitly
reuses the commands already given for full-distance marching and halting
(¶335, ¶337).

**Sequence of actions:**
- A column at half distance or in mass, halted, is put in march by the
  **same commands** prescribed for full distance (¶219) (¶335).
- Means of direction (markers, guide-tracing, etc.) are likewise identical
  for half-distance/mass columns as for full distance; if in mass, the
  junior major occupies the post prescribed at ¶259 (route-column post)
  (¶336).
- Halting a marching half-distance/mass column uses the same commands as
  halting a full-distance column (¶286); if a general guide-realignment is
  then wanted, use ¶290ff's commands/means (¶337).
- In half-distance/mass columns, chiefs of subdivision still repeat `march`
  and `halt` exactly as in full-distance columns (¶338).
- Colonel will often march the column to the rear using the means/commands
  of ¶225–226 (the about-while-marching drill from Article I) (¶339).
- A column by division or company — at full distance, half distance, **or**
  mass, halted **or** marching — can be faced right/left and marched off in
  the new direction (¶340) — i.e., a simple change of front for the whole
  column, distinct from the wheeling "change direction in column" of
  Article III.
- If skirmisher companies are present in this case, platoon columns and the
  battalion column are directed per Nos. 140, 141, 142 (¶341, cross-refs
  not chased).

**Remarks (¶342, "0-342"):** When one or more skirmisher platoons are
deployed to screen a battalion marching in column-by-company-or-division at
half distance, and the colonel recalls them (not to form square), execution
follows No. 95 (cross-ref, not chased) — except that the platoon columns,
on reaching their positions, close **in mass** instead of keeping full
distance.

**Start/end state:** Column already at half distance or closed in mass,
halted → marching at the same (unchanged) distance, in the original or a
newly faced direction; or marching → halted, same distance preserved
throughout.

**Distances:** No new distances introduced — this article is definitionally
about the *invariance* of half-distance/mass spacing under march/halt/face
operations (the spacing set by Article V persists through all of Article
VI's operations).

**Cross-references noted, not chased:** ¶94 (junior major's general
post, re-cited), Nos. 140–142 (¶341), No. 95 (¶342), self-references to
¶219, ¶225–226, ¶259, ¶286, ¶290ff.

**Complexity notes:** This article requires no new geometry — it's a
composability guarantee: every march/halt/about/face operation from
Articles I and IV works unchanged regardless of which distance state
(full/half/mass) the column is currently in. For the engine, this confirms
that `distance` should be a persistent property of the column's state
object, untouched by march/halt/wheel/about operations — only Article V's
"close column" operation (and, presumably, a later "take distances" article
beyond ¶342, reopening the column) should mutate it. Good confirmation that
the distance parameter and the march/halt functions are orthogonal in the
engine design.
