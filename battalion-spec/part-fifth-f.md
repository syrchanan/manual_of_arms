## 1. Reduce square (¶1086–1088)

**Title & range:** ¶1086–1088, no sub-heading in source (falls under Article XIV's
running "dispositions against cavalry" material, immediately before the
"REMARKS ON THE RALLYING OF SKIRMISHERS" sub-heading at ¶1089).

**Exact commands** (¶1086):
> 1. Reduce square. 2. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions:**
- "This movement will be executed in the manner indicated No. 1060 and following"
  (¶1087) — i.e., it reuses the baseline "break the square, re-form column"
  mechanics from the sibling spec's range wholesale. Only the deltas below are
  new to this command form.
- Delta 1 (¶1087): the file closers of the **fourth front** place themselves
  behind the rear rank the instant it faces about (a timing note — they don't
  wait for the rest of the reduction).
- Delta 2 (¶1087): the field and staff, the color-bearer, and the music
  simultaneously return to their column posts.
- Delta 3 (¶1088, skirmisher paragraph, `0-1088`): the junior major faces the
  skirmishers to the right; each platoon-column chief and guide re-posts per
  ¶879; at the march, the first platoon column moves diagonally to the front
  and the second diagonally to the rear, back to their flank-of-column
  positions.

**Start/end state:** Start = battalion in square (any of the forms this spec or
the sibling spec covers). End = battalion back in column by company/division,
identical in form to whatever column type it was ployed from (per ¶1060ff,
owned by sibling spec).

**Distances/measurements:** None new — inherits ¶1060ff.

**Cross-references:** No. 1060 and following (baseline reduce-square, sibling
spec's range).

**Complexity notes:** No new geometry — this is the command that *invokes* the
baseline reduce-square mechanic; it is listed here only because the command
name itself ("Reduce square") and its two timing deltas (file-closer and
color/music re-posting) fall numerically at ¶1086–1088, just inside this
spec's assigned start point.

## 3. To form square from line of battle — general principle (¶1090–1092)

**Title & range:** ¶1090–1092, source sub-heading "To form square from line of
battle."

**Content (no commands yet — this is the general/orienting paragraph before the
specific command forms in sections 4–9 below):**
- ¶1090: "A battalion deployed may be formed into square, in a direction
  either parallel or perpendicular to the line of battle." This is the master
  distinction for this whole group of sub-topics — squares formed **from
  column** (sibling spec's baseline, ¶999ff) always start from column; the
  sections here start from **line of battle** (deployed), and the battalion's
  facing relative to its original line-of-battle front is a designer choice
  (parallel or perpendicular).
- ¶1091 (**parallel case**): colonel breaks the battalion by division to the
  rear, by the right or left, then closes the column to half distance "as
  indicated No. 1029 and following" (cross-ref, not chased — likely owned by
  an earlier spec covering ¶1019–1059-ish, i.e. sibling's range).
- ¶1092 (**perpendicular case**): colonel ploys the battalion into simple
  column by division at half distance in rear of the right or left division,
  OR into column doubled on the centre, OR forms square forward on the two
  centre companies. These three sub-options are spelled out as separate
  command forms in sections 4/6/8 below.

**Start/end state:** Start = battalion in line of battle (deployed), not
column — this is the key structural difference from the sibling spec's
baseline (which always starts from column by company). End state (per
sub-option) = square, oriented either parallel (long axis along the original
line-of-battle front) or perpendicular (long axis along the original line's
depth/facing direction) to the original line of battle.

**Distances/measurements:** None new at this orienting level; see sections 4–9.

**Cross-references:** No. 1029 and following (parallel-case break/close,
outside this spec's range — likely sibling's).

**Complexity notes:** This establishes that "form square" needs a
**line-of-battle entry point**, not just a column entry point — i.e., the
engine's square-formation primitive should accept a starting formation type
(column vs. line) as a parameter, with the line-of-battle path first ploying
into one of three intermediate column shapes (flank-division column, double
column, or a forward-on-centre split) before the shared column→square
mechanics apply.

## 4. Ploy into column on a flank division, to form square (¶1093–1095)

**Title & range:** ¶1093–1095 (no distinct sub-heading; continues "To form
square from line of battle").

**Exact commands** (¶1093):
> 1. To form square. 2. Column at half distance by division. 3. On the first
> (or fourth) division. 4. Battalion right (or left)—FACE. 5. MARCH (or double
> quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶1094: "executed according to the principles prescribed No. 159 and
  following" — i.e., this is exactly the Part Second ployment-into-column-by-
  division mechanic (already implemented, per `TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s Phase
  B1 completion notes referencing `columnOfCompanies`), with one delta: **the
  division next to the leading one takes its distance from the rear rank of
  the one in front** (a half-distance spacing rule, consistent with "half
  distance" named in the command).
- ¶1095 (`0-1095`, skirmisher paragraph): companies of skirmishers ploy per
  ¶161/¶164; flagged, not chased.

**Start/end state:** Start = line of battle, halted. End = simple column by
division at half distance, right (or left) in front, standing on the named
flank division — i.e., an ordinary half-distance column, identical in shape to
what Part Second/Third already produce. This is *not itself* the square; it's
the ploy step that sets up the shared column→square mechanic from the sibling
spec's ¶999ff baseline to run next (implied but not stated as a separate
command here — the colonel would issue "Form square..." per ¶999 afterward).

**Distances/measurements:** Half distance (division next to leading one takes
distance from rear rank of the one in front — same half-distance rule as Part
Third's column, ¶201-ish precedent).

**Cross-references:** No. 159 and following (Part Second ploy-into-column,
already implemented); implicitly chains into No. 999ff (sibling's baseline
square-from-column).

**Complexity notes:** No new geometry — this is a direct reuse of the existing
`columnOfCompanies`/ploy-into-column primitive as a **precursor stage** before
the square primitive runs. Confirms the "form square from line" drill should
be modeled as two chained sub-movements (ploy, then square), not one novel
geometry function.

## 5. Same, battalion marching, perpendicular square (¶1096–1099)

**Title & range:** ¶1096–1099 (no distinct sub-heading; continues the same
topic while the battalion is marching rather than halted).

**Exact commands** (¶1096):
> 1. To form square. 2. On the first (or fourth) division, form column.
> 3. Battalion by the right (or left) flank. 4. MARCH (or double
> quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶1097: executed "according to the principle prescribed for ploying a column
  by division at half distance, No. 201." The chief of the first division
  halts his division at the command march.
- ¶1098 (`0-1098`, skirmisher): companies of skirmishers execute as if from a
  halt; flagged, not chased.
- ¶1099: a **command-substitution note**, not a new drill — "If the colonel
  should command, *prepare for square*, instead of *to form square*, the chief
  of the leading subdivision will command quick time, and at the command
  march... the leading subdivision will march in quick time." I.e., swapping
  the preparatory command word changes only the gait (quick time vs. whatever
  gait was already in use), not the geometry.

**Start/end state:** Start = battalion **marching** in line of battle
(perpendicular-square case, i.e. this is the marching counterpart to the
"perpendicular" branch of ¶1092). End = same half-distance column by division
as section 4, reached without an intervening halt.

**Distances/measurements:** Half distance (No. 201 precedent, same as
section 4).

**Cross-references:** No. 201 (Part Third ploy-while-marching, already
implemented); No. 1096–1098 self-contained.

**Complexity notes:** Same underlying ploy primitive as section 4, but
triggered from a marching start rather than a halt — a timing/gating
difference (no halt-then-face beat), not new geometry. The "prepare for
square" vs. "to form square" gait distinction (¶1099) is a command-label /
narration detail, not a keyframe-geometry difference — worth capturing in
`reenactorNotes` rather than a separate `subMovement`, consistent with how
Phase B1 handled other marching-variant timing differences (per
`TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s "Notable implementation decisions").

## 8. Form square, forward on the centre companies (¶1106–1122)

**Title & range:** ¶1106–1122, no distinct sub-heading (continues "To form
square from line of battle"; this is the third option named at ¶1092 and by
far the most detailed of the three).

**Exact commands** (¶1106):
> 1. Forward on the centre, form square. 2. Battalion, inward face.
> 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions (at a halt):**
- **Setup, first command** (¶1107): lieutenant-colonel hastens to the front
  and places **three markers**, at division-or-company distance from the line
  (distance depends on whether the battalion has 8 or 6 companies): marker 1
  opposite the right file of the right-centre company; marker 2 opposite one
  of the three left files of the left-centre company; marker 3 opposite one of
  the three left files of the right-centre company. Every captain steps two
  paces in front of his company's centre; the two centre-company captains
  caution their companies to stay faced front; the rest of the right wing
  cautions to face left, the rest of the left wing cautions to face right.
- ¶1108: music (two ranks) posts at platoon distance behind the inner platoons
  of the two centre companies; color-bearer steps back into file closers
  (replaced per ¶1005, cross-ref to sibling's baseline); covering sergeants
  step into the front rank.
- ¶1109 (`0-1109`, skirmisher): platoon-column chiefs conform to ¶877;
  flagged.
- **Second command** (¶1110): the two centre companies stay faced front; the
  rest of the right wing faces left, the rest of the left wing faces right.
  Captains of newly-faced companies hasten to the (per ¶806/¶809) flank of
  their own company. Captains of the companies **immediately adjacent** to the
  centre companies caution that *they* will wheel by file right/left
  respectively; every other faced captain (except the outermost flank
  companies) cautions his company to "follow in the trace of its leading
  file" — i.e., a **file-following chain**, not a synchronized wheel, cascades
  outward from the centre.
- ¶1111: captains of the **outermost right and left companies** caution them
  to move straight forward (not wheel at all); their file closers swing around
  to the front rank at their eventual line-of-battle position. The senior
  captain of the two centre companies stands before his division's centre and
  cautions it to move forward; junior captain posts per ¶880.
- ¶1112 (`0-1112`, skirmisher): platoon columns execute per ¶879; flagged.
- **March command** (¶1113): each captain rapidly repeats "march." First
  division's chief commands "Guide right"; the right guide steers so the
  right file's front-rank man's breast contacts the right marker's elbow. The
  left-centre company's left guide moves to its own company's left; the guide
  of the company right of the right-centre company moves into the front rank
  at its left flank as soon as it can pass.
- ¶1114: lieutenant-colonel positions to face the left guide of the (centre)
  division on arrival; senior major mirrors for the right guide. The captain
  of the company next-right-of-centre conducts it so his left guide follows
  the trace of the centre division's right file; the company next-left-of-
  centre mirrors on the left. First and last companies march straight forward
  throughout.
- **Halt** (¶1115): "when the centre division has arrived near the markers in
  front," colonel commands: `1. Battalion, by the right and left flanks.
  2. Halt.`
- ¶1116: at "halt," first division halts and dresses right; its outer files
  face outward; file closers close one pace on the rear rank. Companies that
  wheeled by file right now face by the right flank, their right guides
  stepping out and covering the first division's right guide (assured by
  senior major); companies that wheeled left mirror (assured by
  lieutenant-colonel). File closers of the second/third fronts close one pace.
- ¶1117: captains of first/last companies continue marching their companies
  at the first command; at halt they personally halt and watch their own
  company file past until nearly united, then command their company to face
  by the flank and halt — i.e., the two outer/flank companies form the fourth
  front by **filing past** the halted formation and closing the gap, closing
  the square from the rear.
- ¶1118: fourth division aligns by the rear rank per ¶1011 (sibling's
  baseline); junior captain and left company's covering sergeant post per that
  same paragraph; file closers close one pace, outer files face outward.
- ¶1119 (`0-1119`, skirmisher): platoon columns move diagonally to the front
  in double-quick/run to a position behind the inner platoons of the first
  front, at company-front-plus-4-paces distance; flagged.
- ¶1120: junior major posts as at ¶1003 (sibling's baseline).
- ¶1121: square formed, colonel commands `Guides—POSTS.`
- ¶1122: dispositions made "as prescribed No. 1014 and following" (sibling's
  baseline guide-posting sequence).

**Start/end state:** Start = line of battle, halted, facing forward. End =
standard two-rank square whose **first front is the two former centre
companies plus the divisions that wheeled onto them**, and whose **fourth
front is closed last, from the rear, by the two original flank companies
filing in and meeting**. This is geometrically the most distinctive variant in
this spec: instead of a column peeling into four fronts symmetrically from one
end (baseline) or from a ploy stage (sections 4–7), the square here **grows
outward from the centre of the original line**, split into two wings that
wheel oppositely (right wing left, left wing right) while the centre marches
straight ahead to become the leading front, and the two flank companies swing
around independently to close the far side.

**Distances/measurements:** Markers placed at division-or-company distance
from the original line (exact pace count depends on 8 vs. 6 company battalion
— text does not give a fixed pace figure here, unlike the oblique-square
markers in section 20 which do specify "twelve paces"). Platoon columns end at
company-front-plus-4-paces (¶1119, matching the general 4-pace figure used
elsewhere for platoon-column-behind-division spacing, e.g. ¶1003/¶1417 area).
File closers close 1 pace on the rear rank (¶1116, ¶1118) — consistent with
other square-forming file-closer closures in the sibling's baseline.

**Cross-references:** ¶806, ¶809 (captain posting on a faced company's flank —
outside this spec's range); ¶880 (junior captain posting); ¶1005, ¶1011,
¶1014ff, ¶1003 (sibling's baseline square mechanics, reused wholesale for the
color-bearer swap, fourth-front alignment, guide posting, and junior-major
post respectively).

**Complexity notes:** This is the **most novel geometry in this spec's
range**. It requires:
1. A **bidirectional-wheel-from-centre** primitive — two wings of the
   original line wheel in opposite directions (mirrored, like the
   `alternatingPivotWheel`/bidirectional-peel primitives already built in
   Phase B1 for the interior-division countermarch/deploy cases per
   `TODO-battalion-plan.md`, but starting from a *line*, not a column, and
   with the two centre companies staying stationary-facing as the pivot/seed
   of the first front rather than being one of the wheeling elements).
2. A **file-chain-following cascade** distinct from a synchronized wheel
   (¶1110: "follow in the trace of its leading file") for the companies
   between the centre and the outermost flank companies — likely reusable via
   the existing `cascadeBlend()` helper (built in Phase B2 for Article XI
   per the plan doc), but applied to a wheeling geometry rather than a
   straight-file-forming one, so may need adaptation rather than direct
   reuse.
3. A **late-closing fourth front by filing-past-and-meeting** (¶1117) — the
   two outer flank companies march straight through the forming square's
   interior/rear and meet in the middle to close the last side, rather than
   simply wheeling into place like a baseline division. This is a distinct
   choreography from anything in the sibling's baseline square or Phase B1's
   deployment primitives; likely needs its own small helper (two companies
   converging along parallel paths to a shared midpoint, timed to arrive
   after the other three fronts are set).

## 10. Squares in four ranks — general (¶1126)

**Title & range:** ¶1126, source sub-heading "Squares in four ranks."

**Content:** "If the square formed in two ranks, according to the preceding
rules, should not be deemed sufficiently strong, the colonel may cause the
square to be formed in four ranks." Pure orienting statement — the tactical
rationale (a deeper, four-rank face is a stronger defense against a cavalry
charge) for the whole group of sections 11–19 below.

**Start/end state:** N/A (no movement).

**Complexity notes:** Establishes that four-rank squares are a **defensive-
strength variant**, not a different formation family — every command form
that follows (sections 11–19) mirrors a two-rank counterpart 1:1 (from
column, from line, on a flank division, forward on centre, marching or
halted) with the delta always being "each company doubles its files to form
four ranks instead of two" at the appropriate step. Confirms the engine
should model rank-count (2 vs. 4) as an orthogonal parameter to the
square-entry-path parameter identified in section 3, not a separate set of
formation functions.

## 11. Form square in four ranks, from column by company, at a halt (¶1127–1132)

**Title & range:** ¶1127–1132 (no distinct sub-heading; first concrete command
form under "Squares in four ranks").

**Exact commands** (¶1127):
> 1. To form square in four ranks. 2. To half distance, close column.
> 3. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions:**
- Precondition: "The battalion being in column by company at half or full
  distance, right in front, and at a halt... he will first cause divisions to
  be formed" (¶1127) — same precondition as the two-rank baseline (sibling's
  ¶999).
- ¶1128: at the first command, first division's right company faces left, left
  company faces right (this pre-faces the two companies that will double onto
  each other to form the first front's four ranks); other divisions' chiefs
  caution to move forward.
- ¶1129 (`0-1129`, skirmisher): platoon columns execute per ¶1002 with a new
  delta — "the chief of the first platoon column, the moment it has its place
  in rear of the division next to the last, will break to the rear **three
  files** from the right flank of each platoon," mirrored on the left for the
  second platoon column. This "break to the rear three files" pattern recurs
  as a cross-reference in several later skirmisher paragraphs in this spec
  (¶1146, ¶1150, ¶1155) — flagged, not chased (skirmisher-scoped), but noted
  because it's the canonical four-rank skirmisher adaptation referenced
  repeatedly.
- ¶1130: at the march, **first division's right company doubles into four
  ranks on its left file; left company doubles into four ranks on its right
  file**. This is the core new mechanic: each company folds its own two ranks
  into four by doubling files inward from its outer flank onto an inner pivot
  file, per-company, not battalion-wide. Once doubled, the chief aligns the
  division left.
- ¶1131: the **other divisions** move forward and double their files **while
  marching** (right company doubles on its left file, left company on its
  right file — same pattern as ¶1130). Each chief commands "Guide left" once
  doubled; each division halts when it reaches "a company front in four ranks"
  distance from the one ahead (measured from the rear rank of the division in
  front), then aligns left. When the fourth division halts, file closers move
  rapidly to its front rank.
- ¶1132: from here, "The colonel will form square, re-form column, and reduce
  square in four ranks, by the same commands and means as prescribed for a
  battalion in two ranks" — i.e., once this four-rank column is closed, the
  actual square-forming/reducing mechanic is **identical** to the sibling's
  two-rank baseline (¶999ff/¶1060ff), just operating on four-rank-deep
  divisions instead of two-rank-deep ones.

**Start/end state:** Start = column by company, half or full distance, right
in front, halted, divisions formed. End = a four-rank-deep close column at
"company front in four ranks" spacing, ready for the shared column→square
mechanic to run (same handoff pattern as sections 4/6 above, but with a
file-doubling step inserted).

**Distances/measurements:** Half distance to close column (command name);
division spacing = "the distance of a company front in four ranks" (i.e., the
width of a company when doubled to 4 ranks — narrower/deeper footprint than
the 2-rank company front used elsewhere).

**Cross-references:** No. 999ff, No. 1060ff (sibling's baseline square
mechanics, reused unmodified once the column is four-ranks-deep). S.C. cross-
refs to company-level file-doubling (implied — this is the same doubling
mechanic as School of Company's file-doubling, applied per-company at
battalion scale).

**Complexity notes:** New primitive needed: **per-company file-doubling
applied to specific companies within a moving/halting battalion column**,
where the doubling pivot file is always the file nearest the division's own
guide/centre (right company doubles onto its *left* file, left company onto
its *right* file — i.e., both double **inward**, toward the division seam).
This is analogous to School of Company's existing file-doubling mechanic
(`doubleFiles()`/`undoubleFiles()` in `formations.js`) but needs to be
triggered **mid-march**, per-company, with a directional (left-pivot vs.
right-pivot) parameter — likely a battalion-scale wrapper around the existing
per-soldier `doubleFiles()` rather than new geometry from scratch.

## 14. Battalion in line, form square in four ranks on a flank division (¶1141–1146)

**Title & range:** ¶1141–1146 (no distinct sub-heading; this is the four-rank
counterpart of section 4).

**Exact commands** (¶1141):
> 1. To form square, in four ranks. 2. Column at half distance, by division.
> 3. On the first (or fourth) division. 4. Battalion, right (or left)—FACE.
> 5. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶1142: at the second command, each chief of division stands before his
  division's centre and cautions it to face right.
- ¶1143: at the fourth command, the **right guide of the first division stays
  faced front** (doesn't face with the rest — he's the anchor/pivot point);
  battalion faces right.
- ¶1144: at the march, **the first file of four men of the first division**
  faces front, remaining doubled (the leading division is pre-doubled into
  four-rank files before the march step, unlike section 11 where doubling
  happens progressively division-by-division while entering column — here
  doubling into 4-man files happens first, then the column forms from
  already-doubled files). All other 4-man files step off together and each in
  turn closes to its proper distance on the file ahead, facing front,
  remaining doubled. When the last file closes, the chief commands
  `Left—DRESS.`
- ¶1145: the other divisions ploy into column the same way as the two-rank
  battalion (section 4's mechanic), with a delta: chiefs of division
  **continue leading their divisions** rather than letting them file past (a
  posture difference from the two-rank case); as each division reaches the
  first division's right guide's line, its chief halts the right guide, who
  faces front; the first 4-man file halts and faces front remaining doubled;
  the second file closes on the first, halts, faces front, remaining doubled;
  all others follow suit successively. Last file closed → `Left—DRESS.`
- ¶1146 (`0-1146`, skirmisher): companies of skirmishers execute per ¶1095
  (section 4's skirmisher note), breaking files to the rear per ¶1129
  (section 11's three-file break); flagged.

**Start/end state:** Start = line of battle, halted. End = four-rank column by
division at half distance on the named flank division — four-rank analog of
section 4's end state, with files already doubled into 4-man units before
column-entry rather than doubled after.

**Distances/measurements:** Half distance; "proper distance" between 4-man
files not given a pace figure here (implicitly the standard file-closing
interval).

**Cross-references:** Two-rank counterpart = section 4 (No. 159ff). This
paragraph range doesn't itself cross-reference 159ff by number, but the
structural parallel is exact per the "Squares in four ranks" framing (¶1126).

**Complexity notes:** A **files-of-four** primitive distinct from section 11's
per-company doubling-while-marching-into-column: here, individual 4-man file
units are the thing that steps off, closes distance, and dresses — i.e., the
engine needs to treat a "file of four" as an atomic marching/closing unit
(similar to how individual files already act as units in the existing
company-scale flank-march mechanic), generalized to battalion-scale division
entry. Likely reuses the existing per-file marching mechanic from School of
Company's flank-march work, but chained into the division-ploy primitive.

## 20. Oblique squares (¶1167–1181)

**Title & range:** ¶1167–1181, source sub-heading "Oblique squares."

### 20a. From line of battle, on a flank division (¶1167–1173)

**Exact commands** (¶1167):
> 1. To form oblique square. 2. On the first division form, column.

(note: only two numbered preparatory parts given initially; the execution
command `3. Battalion right—FACE. 4. MARCH` follows at ¶1169 after the
marker-tracing interlude — this command is split across two paragraphs in the
source, unlike the single-block commands elsewhere in this spec.)

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶1168 (**marker-tracing, at the second command**): the lieutenant-colonel
  traces the new alignment: stands before/near the right file of the first
  division, faces left, marches **12 paces** along the front rank, halts,
  faces right, marches **12 paces perpendicular to the front**, halts, faces
  right, places a marker. The right company's covering sergeant simultaneously
  steps before its right file, faces left, and squares his shoulder-line to
  the lieutenant-colonel's marker. A third marker is placed on the same
  alignment at the point where the division's left will halt.
- ¶1169: chiefs of division post before their divisions' centres; the first
  division's chief wheels it right on a fixed pivot against the markers and
  aligns it left (i.e., the whole division rotates onto the new 45°-offset
  line via a fixed-pivot wheel against the staked markers — this is the
  mechanism that actually produces the obliquity). Other chiefs caution
  face-right. Colonel then commands the execution: `3. Battalion
  right—FACE. 4. MARCH (or double quick—MARCH).`
- ¶1170: the three rear divisions direct their march to place themselves at
  half distance from each other, in rear of the first division, as previously
  indicated (i.e., ploying into an ordinary half-distance column — but behind
  a division that is now on the oblique line).
- ¶1171: **second division's** chief breaks the headmost files **to the
  front** (not to the rear, as in the ordinary/baseline ployment) and
  conducts the division toward the column's entrance point; on arrival he
  halts personally, wheels his division by file right (right guide directed
  parallel to the first division), and once the left file passes, halts and
  aligns left. The **other divisions** break to the rear only slightly, each
  entering the column the same way as the second; once ployed into column,
  colonel forms square (chains into sibling's baseline column→square
  mechanic).
- ¶1172: forming oblique square on the **left** division uses the same
  principles by inverse means (mirror image).
- ¶1173: if the battalion is marching, the colonel first halts it (no
  oblique-square-while-marching-into-column variant given for this sub-case —
  contrast with 20b below, which does have a marching variant).

**Start/end state:** Start = line of battle, halted (or marching, halted
first per ¶1173). End = square whose entire footprint is rotated **45°** (an
oblique, i.e. diagonal, angle) relative to the original line-of-battle
orientation — this is the headline geometric novelty of this whole
sub-section: not a different rank depth or entry path, but a **whole-square
rotation**. The fixed-pivot wheel of the first division (¶1169) against
markers placed via the 12-pace/12-pace perpendicular staking (¶1168) is the
mechanism; subsequent divisions ploy into column behind the now-obliqued
first division, so the entire resulting square inherits the 45°-ish offset
(note: the source doesn't literally say "45 degrees" anywhere in this range —
it only specifies the two 12-pace legs of the marker-tracing, which happen to
form a right isosceles triangle implying a 45° angle at the pivot; flagged as
an **inference from the geometry described, not an explicit source
statement** — worth double-checking against any diagram if the original PDF
has one, since this project's extraction is text-only).

**Distances/measurements:** 12 paces along the front rank, then 12 paces
perpendicular to the front (¶1168) — these two equal legs are what produce
the oblique angle. Half distance for the trailing divisions' column (¶1170).

**Cross-references:** Chains into sibling's baseline column→square mechanic
once the oblique column is formed.

**Complexity notes:** Requires a **fixed-pivot wheel against staked markers**
primitive — distinct from the alternating-pivot wheel already built in Phase
B1 (that one is for in-place countermarching; this one rotates a single
division onto a new heading using two perpendicular 12-pace reference legs,
more like a compass/protractor construction than a wheel-in-formation). Also
requires the whole square-formation pipeline (whichever entry path is chosen)
to accept an **arbitrary rotation offset** parameter rather than assuming the
square's fronts are always axis-aligned with the original line or column
heading — likely the single biggest architectural implication in this spec:
if squares are currently modeled as always facing the same direction as their
parent column/line, oblique squares break that assumption and need a
rotation parameter threaded through the whole pipeline.

### 20b. From column, by changing direction (¶1174–1181)

**Exact commands** (¶1174, first part):

- ¶1174: if the battalion is *already* in column (rather than deployed line,

  change direction — reused here, not re-derived).
- ¶1175: lieutenant-colonel traces the new direction: places markers before

  prolongation on the side of the direction-change at **12 paces** from the
  column's flank, then paces 12 more perpendicular to front from that third

- ¶1176: colonel commands the execution (`3. Battalion right (or
  left)—FACE. 4. MARCH...`).
- ¶1177: once the change of direction is executed, colonel forms the square

- ¶1178: if the column is marching, colonel first halts it.
- ¶1179: **oblique squares in four ranks** use the same means/principles as
  the four-rank squares already described (sections 11–19) — no separate

- ¶1180: whichever ploy (simple or double column) is used, the particular

- ¶1181 (`0-1181`, skirmisher): companies of skirmishers conform to the

maneuver instead of a marker-staked wheel from line.

**Distances/measurements:** Same 12-pace/12-pace staking as 20a.

**Cross-references:** Change of direction by the flank (Part Fifth Art. XII,
already implemented per `TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s Phase B2/earlier-phase
scope — confirm exact drill name in that Part's own spec, not chased here).
No. 1000ff (sibling baseline).

**Complexity notes:** Reuses the existing change-of-direction primitive to
establish the oblique heading (lower complexity than 20a's marker-staked
wheel), but still needs the same "square pipeline accepts a rotation
parameter" architectural change flagged in 20a. The senior major's
guide-position correction (¶1180) is a minor annotation/detail-level fixup,

- **¶1182**: For squares formed from **6-company** columns specifically, the
  music posts one pace in rear of the second division's file closers (instead
  of the 8-company placement described elsewhere); for centre-forward squares
  from line, music posts one pace in rear of the first division's file
  closers. **Note:** this project's battalion model defaults to 8 companies
  (`TODO-battalion-plan.md`) — the 6-company case is flagged as a
  configuration variant, not chased.
- **¶1183**: General principle — a column by company forming square normally
  first forms divisions and closes to half distance; but if surprised by
  cavalry without time for that, the colonel closes to **platoon distance**
  instead and forms square directly, with lead/rear companies playing the
  division role and other companies forming by platoon into line, each
  platoon chief acting as if his platoon were a company (covered by the
  rear-rank guide). This is effectively a **fifth entry path** (platoon-
  distance emergency variant) parallel to the ones in sections 4–9, flagged
  as a distinct case worth its own drill entry if implemented, not merely a
  footnote.
- **¶1184** (`0-1184`, skirmisher): mechanics for skirmisher companies under
  the platoon-distance case; flagged, not chased.
- **¶1185**: Restates general column-closing rules already covered elsewhere
  (full-distance column always closes on the leading subdivision; mass-closed
  column takes distances by the head; second subdivision always measures
  from the rear rank of the one ahead). No new geometry.
- **¶1186**: For four-rank squares specifically, file-doubling **always**
  happens on the file next the guide — confirms/generalizes the per-company
  doubling direction convention used throughout sections 11–19 (doubling is
  always guide-adjacent, i.e., inward toward the division's own guide, not an
  arbitrary choice).
- **¶1187** (`0-1187`, skirmisher): flagged, not chased.
- **¶1188**: A column disposed to form square, while marching, changes
  direction using the **half-distance column** change-of-direction mechanic
  (already implemented); if the guide isn't already on the side opposite the
  direction change, the column takes the guide on that opposite side first.
  Procedural note, not new geometry.
- **¶1189**: A column doubled on the centre (at company distance, or closed in
  mass) forms square by the **same principles** as a simple column — confirms
  no separate geometry needed for that column type beyond what sections 6–7
  and the sibling's Article XIII already provide.
- **¶1190**: Doctrine/rationale note — squares ployed with a view to defense
  are always formed in rear of a flank division (not centre), so the
  battalion can keep firing during the ploy; double column is preferred where
  circumstances allow, since it forms fastest. Doctrine, not geometry.
- **¶1191, ¶1192**: Firing doctrine while in square (fire by file/by rank; the
  color guard doesn't fall back; in four-rank squares only the front two ranks
  fire, the rear two stay at shoulder/support arms). Not a movement/geometry
  concern — flagged as a possible static annotation for a four-rank square
  drill's `reenactorNotes` (which ranks are "active"), not a keyframe.
- **¶1193, ¶1194**: General doctrine on square formation being complex and
  requiring frequent drill/care against precipitancy. Narrative, not geometry.
- **¶1195–1200**: Skirmisher-rally-specific procedures for how skirmishers
  re-enter a formed square (angles opened to receive them, or skirmishers
  kneeling at the front rank's feet if angles can't be opened, platoon/section
  deployment out of square interiors, recall by sounding "the color"). All
  skirmisher-scoped — flagged, not chased, consistent with project's
  no-skirmisher-companies scope decision.

**Complexity notes:** The one item here with real implementation weight if
pursued is **¶1183's platoon-distance emergency square** — a distinct entry
path not covered in sections 4–9 or the sibling's baseline, using platoons
(not companies/divisions) as the folding unit for the two end formations.
Everything else in this range is doctrine/narrative or skirmisher-scoped.

## 22. Column against cavalry (¶1201–1211)

**Title & range:** ¶1201–1211, source sub-heading "Column against cavalry."
This is explicitly presented as an **alternative to** forming a full square
under extreme time pressure — a distinct, simpler defensive formation, not a
square variant.

**Precondition** (¶1201): "When a column closed in mass has to form square, it
will begin by taking company distance, but if so suddenly threatened by
cavalry as not to allow time for this disposition, it will be formed [into
column-against-cavalry] in the following manner."

**Exact commands** (¶1202):
> 1. Column against cavalry. 2. MARCH.

**Sequence of actions:**
- ¶1203: at the first command, the **leading division** stands fast and its
  file closers pass behind the rear rank. In **interior divisions**, each
  captain designates enough files to close the interval between his own
  company and the one ahead of it. The captains of the division next-to-last
  additionally close the interval separating that division from the very
  last one. The **fourth (rearmost) division**'s chief cautions it to face
  about; its file closers pass briskly in front of the front rank.
- ¶1204 (`0-1204`, skirmisher): platoon-column chiefs dispose along the
  front/right flank and rear/left flank of the battalion column per ¶1196
  (cross-ref, likely a typo/renumbering in source for the square-flank-
  disposition principle — not chased further, skirmisher-scoped anyway).
- ¶1205: at the march, division guides move rapidly into the file-closer line.
  First division stands fast, fourth faces about; both divisions' outer files
  face outward. In the other (interior) divisions, the files designated to
  close intervals form to the right/left into line; in the division next to
  the rearmost, the first files reaching the line close right/left until they
  join the rear division. Files remaining in column close on their own
  now-in-line outer files, **creating a vacant space in the column's
  middle** — this vacant interior space is the defining shape of this
  formation (as opposed to a square's four distinct fronts meeting at right-
  angle corners).
- ¶1206: marching-column variant — same commands/means; at the march, first
  and fourth divisions halt (fourth also faces about); interior divisions
  conform as above.
- ¶1207–1208 (**"Form column," the reverse**):
  > 1. Form column. 2. MARCH.

  At the march (¶1208): the files that had been in column close left/right to
  make room for the files that had formed into line, who retake their column
  places by stepping backward — except the files that closed the gap between
  the two rear divisions, who retake their places by a flank movement instead.
  Fourth division faces about; guides resume their posts.
- ¶1209 (`0-1209`, skirmisher): companies of skirmishers retake their places;
  flagged.
- ¶1210: doctrine note — if too pressed to command "fix bayonets" separately,
  the men fix bayonets automatically, without command, at the cautionary
  "column against cavalry."
- ¶1211: doctrine note — recommends frequent drilling of this maneuver given
  its wartime value.

**Start/end state:** Start = column closed in mass (halted or marching), any
number of divisions. End = a **hollow rectangular column**: first division
facing front (unchanged), fourth division faced about (rear-facing), interior
divisions' selected files peeled out to the left/right into a connecting line
along both flanks, leaving a vacant interior. This is geometrically distinct
from every square variant elsewhere in this spec/the sibling's — it's not
four fronts meeting at corners with divisions each occupying one full side;
it's a **column that partially unfolds sideways** while its head and tail stay
column-postured, cavalry-resistant on all sides without ever fully
reconfiguring into the square's four-corner shape. Reduces back to ordinary
column via ¶1207–1208's exact-inverse mechanic.

**Distances/measurements:** No new pace figures given in this range — spacing
is described qualitatively ("close the interval," "vacant space") rather than
with fixed pace counts, unlike most other sections in this spec. Flagged: if
a specific gap width is needed for animation, it isn't specified here and may
need to be inferred from the standard file-interval/company-front figures
already established elsewhere in the project.

**Complexity notes:** A **genuinely distinct third formation shape** (not a
square, not a plain column) requiring its own primitive: partial
sideways-peel of only the *interior* divisions' closing files into two side
lines, while the lead and rear divisions stay column-postured (front-facing
and about-faced respectively) rather than becoming two more full fronts. This
resembles the "bidirectional simultaneous peel" primitive already built in
Phase B1 for interior-division deployment (`TODO-battalion-plan.md`'s
`divisionLineFromAnchor` + `cascadeBlend`), but stops partway (a peel that
only pulls *some* files into line, leaving a "vacant space" rather than
deploying the whole division into a continuous line) — likely adaptable from
that existing primitive with a partial-fill parameter, rather than fully new
geometry. The exact-inverse "Form column" reversal (¶1207–1208) is the
by-file-closing-and-stepping-backward mirror of this peel, distinct from the
baseline "reduce square" mechanic (section 1) since there's no square to
reduce — worth modeling as this formation's own dedicated reverse, not a
call into section 1's reduce-square primitive.
